Raspberry recipes hit differently in the morning. There's something about that bright, slightly tart burst against a sweet, tender crumb that makes a muffin feel worth getting out of bed for.

This version starts with a buttery streusel topping, then builds a soft, lemon-kissed muffin batter studded with fresh raspberries. It comes together in about thirty minutes, no mixer required, and it's the kind of recipe that turns an ordinary weekday morning into something a little more special.

### **Why This Raspberry Muffin Recipe Works**

A lot of muffin recipes skip the streusel and call it a day, but that crumbly, buttery topping is doing real work here. It adds texture contrast against the soft interior and a hit of extra sweetness right where you want it, in that first bite.

The other detail worth calling out is the lemon zest folded into the batter. It's a small addition, just the zest of one lemon, but it lifts the whole flavor of the muffin and makes the raspberries taste even brighter. Raspberry and lemon are a classic pairing for a reason, and this recipe leans into that without turning the muffin into a lemon muffin instead.

### **What You Need Before You Start**

#### **Streusel Topping Ingredients**

* ¼ cup (50 grams) granulated sugar
* ¼ cup (30 grams) all-purpose flour
* A pinch of salt
* 2 tablespoons cold butter, cut into small pieces

#### **Muffin Ingredients**

**Dry ingredients:**

* 2 cups (240 grams) all-purpose flour
* ½ teaspoon salt (or ¼ teaspoon if using fine grain salt)
* 1 cup (200 grams) granulated sugar
* 2 teaspoons baking powder

**Wet ingredients:**

* 5 tablespoons (75 mL) neutral oil
* 3 tablespoons melted butter
* 2 large eggs, room temperature if possible
* ¾ cup (180 mL) milk
* 2 teaspoons vanilla extract
* Zest of 1 lemon

**Mix-in**:

* 1½ cups fresh raspberries

### **How to Make Raspberry Muffins, Step by Step**

#### **Step 1: Make the Streusel Topping**

Combine the sugar, flour, and salt in a small bowl. Add the cold butter pieces and work them into the dry ingredients with your hands until you've got a mix of crumbly pieces, some tiny and some a bit larger. Set the streusel aside while you prep the muffin batter.

#### **Step 2: Mix the Dry Ingredients**

Preheat your oven to 400°F. In a large bowl, whisk together the flour, salt, sugar, and baking powder until evenly combined, then set the bowl aside.

Baking powder is doing specific work here rather than baking soda. It keeps the muffins light instead of adding the deeper browning that baking soda tends to bring to baked goods.

#### **Step 3: Mix the Wet Ingredients**

In a separate bowl, combine the oil and melted butter. Using both together gives you a fluffy crumb from the oil and real butter flavor from the butter, a combination that neither ingredient quite achieves on its own.

Add the eggs and whisk until the mixture looks smooth, breaking up the eggs first if needed. Stir in the milk, vanilla, and lemon zest, whisking everything together until fully combined.

#### **Step 4: Combine and Fold in the Raspberries**

Pour the wet ingredients into the bowl of dry ingredients. Fold the mixture together gently, just until everything is wet and combined. A few visible pockets of flour are exactly what you want at this stage, since overmixing leads to dense, tough muffins.

Add the raspberries and fold them in gently. Raspberries are delicate, so a light hand here keeps them mostly intact instead of streaking the whole batter pink.

#### **Step 5: Fill and Top the Muffins**

Line a muffin tin with paper liners, or leave it ungreased if you prefer a crisper edge on the finished muffins. Both work, and it comes down to personal preference.

Gently transfer the batter into the muffin cups, filling each one and making sure every muffin gets a fair share of raspberries. Sprinkle the streusel topping evenly over each muffin, then top a few with a couple of whole raspberries if you want the fruit to be visible from the outside.

#### **Step 6: Bake**

Bake at 400°F for 18 to 20 minutes, or until the centers are set. A skewer inserted into the middle should come out clean, or close to it, when the muffins are ready. Let them cool slightly in the tin before pulling them out to eat warm.

### **Common Mistakes to Avoid**

**Overmixing the batter.** Stop folding once the flour is just incorporated. A few streaks of dry flour left in the batter are fine and actually help keep the muffins tender.

**Skipping the lemon zest.** It seems like a small ingredient to skip, but it changes the whole flavor of the muffin. The zest brightens the raspberries instead of letting the sweetness dominate.

**Overworking the raspberries**. Fold gently. Stirring too aggressively breaks the berries apart and turns the batter an unappetizing shade of pink instead of leaving those bright bursts of fruit intact.

**Using cold eggs straight from the fridge**. Room temperature eggs blend more smoothly into the wet ingredients. Cold eggs can make the batter look slightly curdled and uneven.

**Underfilling the streusel.** Don't be shy with the topping. It's meant to cover the full surface of each muffin, not just a light dusting in the center.

### **Ways to Customize These Raspberry Recipes**

Once you've got the base muffin down, there's a lot of room to make it your own. Swap the raspberries for blueberries, blackberries, or a mix of all three if you want a different berry profile while keeping the same technique.

The streusel topping is worth experimenting with too. A pinch of cinnamon added to the sugar and flour mixture gives it a warmer, slightly spiced edge that still pairs well with the lemon and raspberries. If you prefer a softer streusel, melted butter instead of cold will give you a slightly different, more cakey crumble instead of the classic sandy texture.

If lemon isn't your favorite, orange zest is a solid substitute that pairs just as well with raspberries, though it shifts the flavor in a slightly sweeter direction. You can also skip the citrus entirely if you'd rather let the raspberries and streusel carry the whole flavor on their own.

For anyone baking ahead, this batter holds up well if you portion it into the muffin tin, cover it, and refrigerate overnight, then bake fresh in the morning. Just add the streusel topping right before baking rather than the night before, so it stays crumbly instead of getting soggy from sitting on top of the batter too long.

### **Conclusion**

Raspberry recipes don't get much better than a warm muffin with a crumbly streusel top and pockets of bright, slightly tart fruit throughout. This one comes together fast enough for a weekday morning but tastes like it took a lot more effort than it actually did.

Make a full batch on the weekend if you want breakfast handled for the next several mornings. These muffins keep well at room temperature for a couple of days, or in the fridge if you want them to last a bit longer. Either way, that first warm bite out of the oven is worth building the recipe around in the first place.
