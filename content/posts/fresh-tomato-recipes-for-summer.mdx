A single tomato plant can produce more fruit than one household knows what to do with. By August, the counter starts to look less like a harvest and more like a problem you need to solve before anything goes soft.

These ten fresh tomato recipes give that surplus somewhere to go. Some take fifteen minutes. Others ask for a little more patience. All of them are built around one idea: when tomatoes are actually at their peak, they don't need much help to taste incredible.

### **Fresh Tomato Recipes Worth Making This Season**

Here's a lineup that ranges from soup to salad to breakfast, so there's a use for however many tomatoes you're currently staring down.

#### **1. Fresh Tomato Soup with Crispy Cheese Toast**

This soup is almost entirely tomato, and that's the whole point. Less filler means more of that vine-ripened flavor coming through in every spoonful.

##### **Ingredients**:

* A mix of ripe tomatoes (early girl, celebrity, cherry, Roma, or whatever you have)
* Butter and olive oil
* Chopped garlic
* 1 anchovy fillet
* Salt and red pepper flakes, to taste
* Italian bread, sliced
* Olive oil, for the bread
* Fresh basil, chiffonade
* Sharp white cheddar, shredded
* A shake of cayenne, optional

##### **Instructions**:

Core and halve larger tomatoes, halve the Romas, and leave cherry tomatoes whole. Melt butter with a little olive oil over medium-high heat, then add garlic and the anchovy fillet, sizzling for about a minute. Add the tomatoes and their juices, season with salt and red pepper flakes, and simmer until they release their liquid. Reduce heat to medium-low and simmer for about 45 minutes, until the tomatoes collapse into a puree. Strain out the skins and seeds, then reheat and taste for seasoning. For the toast, oil a baking sheet, top bread with basil and a generous pile of cheddar, and bake at 450°F for 15 to 20 minutes until browned and crisp.

#### **2. Panzanella Salad**

Traditional panzanella tends to turn soggy fast. This version fries the bread cubes first, so they stay crunchy even after soaking up the dressing.

##### **Ingredients**:

* Half a loaf of stale bread, 2 to 3 days old, cubed
* Olive oil, generously
* Fresh Parmigiano Reggiano, grated
* A variety of tomatoes, halved
* Salt, black pepper, and a pinch of sugar
* Red wine vinegar
* Extra virgin olive oil
* Minced garlic
* Fresh basil, chiffonade

##### **Instructions**:

Toast the bread cubes in a skillet over medium heat with plenty of olive oil until golden and crisp on the outside but still a little chewy inside, then grate Parmesan over them and let them cool completely. Halve the tomatoes and toss with salt, pepper, sugar, red wine vinegar, olive oil, and minced garlic. Cover and let sit for 20 minutes to draw out the juices. Add the basil, adjust the oil and vinegar, then toss in the fried bread cubes. Mix well, let it sit for a few minutes, and check if it needs more dressing before serving.

#### **3. Tomato Tart**

This free-form tart layers sweet summer tomatoes over a Dijon-coated puff pastry crust, and it looks far more impressive than the effort it actually takes.

##### **Ingredients**:

* Puff pastry, thawed
* Dijon mustard, 3 to 4 tablespoons
* Ripe summer tomatoes, sliced about a quarter inch thick
* Herbes de Provence
* Sea salt and black pepper
* Parmigiano Reggiano, finely grated
* Olive oil
* Fresh herbs (parsley, oregano, thyme, chives), for garnish

##### **Instructions**:

Press pieces of puff pastry into a rectangle, add a border from extra strips, then freeze for 10 minutes to firm it up. Slash the edges, dock the bottom with a fork, and bake at 400°F for about 10 minutes until lightly golden. Press down the docked bottom while it's still hot, then let it cool for 10 to 15 minutes. Spread Dijon mustard generously over the base, layer on the tomato slices, and season with salt, pepper, and herbes de Provence. Top with a light dusting of Parmesan and a drizzle of olive oil, then bake at 400°F for 25 to 30 minutes until browned and the tomatoes look caramelized. Cool before slicing, ideally on a rack for a crispier bottom.

#### **4. Fresh Garden Tomato Sauce**

There's no jarred sauce that compares to this. Meaty tomatoes like plum, San Marzano, or Roma work best, since they have more flesh and less juice and seeds.

##### **Ingredients**:

* A mix of meaty tomatoes (plum, San Marzano, or similar), cored and quartered
* Olive oil
* Sliced garlic cloves
* Fresh basil leaves
* Salt and a pinch of chili flakes, optional

##### **Instructions**:

Heat olive oil over medium heat, add sliced garlic, and sizzle for about 30 seconds before adding the basil for another 30 seconds. Add the tomatoes along with any juices, plus salt and chili flakes if using. Raise the heat to medium-high and cook, stirring occasionally, until the tomatoes fully collapse and liquefy. Pass everything through a strainer to remove seeds and skins, then return the sauce to the pot and simmer over medium heat for a few minutes to reach your preferred thickness. Taste and adjust the salt before serving.

#### **5. Naked Cherry Tomato Salad**

Peeling cherry tomatoes sounds like extra work for no reason, until you taste the difference. This one is meant to be served in small portions as a starter.

##### **Ingredients**:

* Cherry tomatoes
* A few mint or basil leaves
* Sherry vinegar (or vinegar of choice)
* Extra virgin olive oil
* A pinch of cayenne or black pepper
* A small pinch of salt, plus coarse sea salt for finishing

##### **Instructions**:

Cut a small, shallow X into the bottom of each tomato, then blanch briefly in boiling water until the skin starts to peel back. Transfer immediately to ice water, then peel off the skins by hand once cooled. Whisk together a light dressing of mint or basil, vinegar, olive oil, pepper, and a small pinch of salt. Spoon the dressing over the peeled tomatoes, cover, and chill for 30 minutes to an hour, tossing gently halfway through. Serve a few per person with a finishing touch of coarse sea salt.

#### **6. Cherry Tomato and Cheese Galette**

A galette is just a free-form tart without a pan, which makes it more forgiving than it looks. This version pairs a cornmeal crust with a tangy goat cheese filling.

##### **Ingredients**:

* All-purpose flour, plus cornmeal
* Salt
* 1 stick unsalted butter, very cold and cubed
* Cider vinegar and ice water
* 8 ounces fresh goat cheese
* 1 egg yolk
* Kosher salt, black pepper, and cayenne
* Fresh basil, sliced
* About 3 cups cherry tomatoes, halved
* Olive oil and 1 teaspoon Dijon mustard
* 1 egg, beaten with water, for egg wash
* Parmigiano Reggiano, grated

##### **Instructions**:

Pulse flour, cornmeal, and salt with cold butter until it resembles coarse crumbs, then add vinegar and ice water and pulse until loose clumps form. Press into a disc, wrap, and chill for an hour. Mix the goat cheese with the egg yolk, salt, pepper, cayenne, and basil. Toss the halved tomatoes with olive oil and Dijon. Roll out the chilled dough, spread the cheese mixture over the center, and top with the tomatoes using a slotted spoon. Fold the edges up and over to form pleats, brush with egg wash, and top with grated Parmesan. Bake at 425°F for about 30 minutes, until deeply browned on top and underneath. Cool completely before slicing.

#### **7. Tomato and Burrata Salad**

Regular mozzarella works fine here, but burrata takes this salad somewhere else entirely. It's rich, creamy, and worth the occasional splurge.

##### **Ingredients**:

* Ripe heirloom tomatoes, thickly sliced
* Burrata cheese
* Fresh basil, torn
* Flaky sea salt and black pepper
* Extra virgin olive oil

##### **Instructions**:

Slice the tomatoes thick using a serrated knife. Layer a slice of tomato, season with salt and pepper, then spoon on some burrata. Tear basil leaves over the top and drizzle generously with olive oil. Repeat the layers until you're out of tomatoes, finishing with a final touch of torn basil. Serve right away while the cheese is still cool and creamy.

#### **8. Fire-Roasted Cherry Tomato Salsa**

Broiling the vegetables before blending adds a smoky depth that raw salsa just doesn't have. This one is a great way to use up an overflow of cherry tomatoes.

##### **Ingredients**:

* Cherry tomatoes
* 1 onion, roughly chopped
* Garlic cloves, skin on
* Jalapeño, sliced
* Olive oil
* Juice of 1 lime
* Salt, black pepper, and cayenne
* A small pinch of dried oregano and ground cumin
* Fresh cilantro leaves

##### **Instructions**:

Arrange the tomatoes, onion, garlic, and jalapeño on an oiled, foil-lined baking sheet. Broil on high, close to the heat, until well charred, turning as needed. Let everything cool slightly, then peel the garlic skins. Transfer everything to a blender along with any accumulated juices, lime juice, salt, pepper, cayenne, oregano, cumin, and cilantro. Pulse until blended to your preferred texture, then refrigerate for a few hours to let the flavors develop before tasting and adjusting the salt.

#### **9. Gazpacho**

Good gazpacho depends entirely on good tomatoes, so save this one for peak season. This version blends part of the mixture smooth while keeping some texture in the rest.

##### **Ingredients**:

* About 1½ pounds ripe tomatoes, peeled and chopped (concassé)
* Half an English cucumber, diced
* Red bell pepper and jalapeño, diced
* Green onion (white and light green parts) and fresh garlic
* Salt, cumin, dried oregano, and black pepper
* A handful of sweet cherry tomatoes
* Lime juice, Worcestershire sauce, and balsamic vinegar
* Olive oil
* Fresh basil, chiffonade, for garnish

##### Instructions:

Peel and chop the larger tomatoes, then combine with the cucumber, bell pepper, jalapeño, green onion, and garlic. Season with salt, cumin, oregano, and pepper, and let it sit to draw out the juices. Separately, blend the cherry tomatoes with lime juice, Worcestershire, balsamic vinegar, and olive oil until smooth, then strain into the chopped mixture. If you like a smoother gazpacho, puree about half the mixture and stir it back in. Chill for at least 2 hours, then taste and adjust the salt before serving cold, topped with basil.

#### **10. Shakshuka**

Technically a dinner that became a breakfast, shakshuka works at any meal. Eggs poach directly in a spiced tomato and pepper sauce until the yolks are just set.

##### **Ingredients**:

* Olive oil
* Diced onion and mushrooms
* Salt
* Diced sweet red bell pepper, a mild red pepper, and 1 sliced jalapeño
* Cumin, paprika, turmeric, cayenne, and black pepper
* Crushed San Marzano tomatoes
* A splash of water
* 4 to 5 eggs
* Feta cheese, crumbled
* Fresh parsley or cilantro
* Toasted bread, for serving

##### **Instructions**:

Cook the onion and mushrooms in olive oil with a pinch of salt over medium-high heat until golden, about 10 minutes. Add the peppers and cook for another 5 minutes until softened. Stir in the spices and cook for a minute or two, then add the crushed tomatoes and a splash of water. Simmer for 15 to 20 minutes, stirring occasionally, and taste for salt. Make small wells in the sauce and crack an egg into each one, ideally from a ramekin to avoid a broken yolk. Season the eggs with salt and pepper, cover, and simmer until the whites are set and the yolks reach your preferred doneness. Finish with feta, a drizzle of olive oil, and fresh herbs, then serve hot with toasted bread.

### **Making the Most of a Tomato Surplus**

Ten recipes is a lot of ground to cover, but that's kind of the point when tomato season hits its stride. A soup solves one pound. A sauce solves another. A tray of shakshuka can handle a dinner's worth on its own.

What ties all of these together is the same idea: fresh, ripe, in-season tomatoes don't need much dressing up. A little salt, a little acid, maybe some cheese or bread alongside, and the tomato does the rest of the work.

If you're staring down a counter full of tomatoes right now, pick whichever one matches your mood and your time. Soup and sauce reward patience. Salads and salsa come together fast. Either way, there's a place on this list for whatever you've got too much of this week.
