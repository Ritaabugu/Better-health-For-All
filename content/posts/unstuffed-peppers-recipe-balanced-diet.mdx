A regular bowl of stuffed pepper casserole is comforting, but it's mostly rice and cheese doing the heavy lifting on volume. That's fine for a Sunday dinner. It's not going to hold you over if you're active, trying to build muscle, or just tired of feeling hungry an hour after eating.

This high-protein unstuffed peppers recipe fixes that without losing what makes the original so good. A leaner beef choice, an extra scoop of beans, and a swap in the cheese department bring the protein count up significantly, and you still get that same one-pan, stuffed-pepper flavor in every bite.

### **Why Protein Matters in This Unstuffed Peppers Recipe**

Rice and beef alone don't offer much staying power. Protein is what slows digestion down and keeps you feeling full for longer, instead of reaching for a snack thirty minutes after dinner. It also gives your body what it needs to repair muscle, which matters whether you had a heavy lift session that morning or just spent all day on your feet at work.

The original stuffed pepper casserole already has a decent base of protein from the ground beef. This version pushes that further by adjusting a few key ingredients rather than overhauling the entire recipe. You're not adding a bunch of protein powder or pretending this is a bodybuilding meal prep container. You're just making smarter swaps.

That's really the whole philosophy behind this unstuffed peppers recipe. Small, realistic changes that add up, not a complete reinvention of a dish you already like.

### **High-Protein Unstuffed Peppers Recipe Ingredients**

Here's what you'll need for one full pan, enough for four to six servings.

* 1 pound lean ground beef (90/10 or leaner)
* 1 red bell pepper, julienned
* 1 yellow bell pepper, julienned
* 1 green bell pepper, julienned
* 1/2 small onion, chopped
* 3 cloves garlic, minced
* 1 (15 oz) can black beans, drained and rinsed
* 1 teaspoon salt, adjusted to taste
* 2 teaspoons ground black pepper
* 2 teaspoons Italian seasoning
* 1 tablespoon Worcestershire sauce
* 1 (15 oz) can Italian diced tomatoes
* 1 1/2 cups beef broth, low sodium if possible
* 3/4 cup long grain rice, uncooked
* 1/2 cup cottage cheese
* 1/2 cup shredded mozzarella cheese
* 1/2 cup shredded extra old cheddar cheese
* Fresh parsley, for garnish

The biggest changes from a standard version are the leaner beef, the added black beans, a smaller amount of rice, and cottage cheese mixed in alongside the mozzarella and cheddar. Each one adds protein without changing the overall flavor much.

### **How to Make This High-Protein Unstuffed Peppers Recipe**

#### **Step 1: Brown the Lean Beef**

Heat a small amount of oil in a large pan over medium high heat. Add your lean ground beef and break it up as it cooks, until it's fully browned through.

Lean beef releases less grease than a fattier blend, so you likely won't need much draining here. If there's excess liquid, drain it off before moving to the next step.

#### **Step 2: Cook the Aromatics and Peppers**

While the beef browns, julienne your three bell peppers into thin strips rather than small dice. The strips hold their shape better through simmering and give you more texture in every bite.

Once the beef is browned, add the chopped onion and minced garlic directly to the pan. Saute for a couple of minutes until fragrant, then stir in the julienned peppers. Cook for another two minutes, just until they start to soften slightly but still have some bite.

#### **Step 3: Season the Mixture**

Stir in the salt, black pepper, Italian seasoning, and Worcestershire sauce. Mix everything together so the seasoning coats the beef, peppers, and aromatics evenly.

This step is doing most of the flavor work, so give it a real stir rather than a quick pass. Worcestershire in particular brings a savory depth that pairs well with the added black beans in the next step.

#### **Step 4: Add Beans, Tomatoes, Broth, and Rice**

Stir in the drained black beans along with the diced tomatoes and beef broth. Bring the mixture to a boil.

Once it's boiling, add your rice. Note that this version uses 3/4 cup instead of a full cup, since the beans are adding volume and protein that the rice was previously covering on its own. Stir everything together so the rice is distributed evenly through the pan.

#### **Step 5: Simmer**

Cover the pan, reduce the heat to low, and let it simmer for about 20 minutes. Halfway through, lift the lid and give everything a quick stir to prevent sticking, then re-cover and continue cooking for the remaining 10 minutes.

By the end of this step, the rice should be tender, the beans should be warmed through, and most of the liquid should be absorbed into the mixture.

#### **Step 6: Stir in Cottage Cheese, Then Add the Rest**

Once the rice is cooked, stir the cottage cheese directly into the pan. It melts down into the mixture and adds a creamy texture along with a solid protein boost, without a strong flavor of its own.

Add half of your mozzarella and half of your cheddar into the pan as well, stirring until melted. Spread the remaining cheese over the top, cover the pan again, and let it melt on low heat for about 10 more minutes.

#### **Step 7: Garnish and Serve**

Once the top layer of cheese has fully melted, let the dish rest for a few minutes before serving. Garnish with fresh parsley for color.

Serve straight from the pan, family style, so everyone can see exactly what's in it.

### **How Much Protein Does This Add?**

Swapping to lean ground beef, adding a full can of black beans, and mixing in cottage cheese alongside the usual cheeses meaningfully increases the protein per serving compared to the original casserole. Beans alone add several grams of both protein and fiber per serving, and cottage cheese is one of the higher protein dairy options available without adding much fat.

The exact numbers will vary depending on your specific brands and portion sizes, but the direction is clear. This version holds you over longer than a rice and cheese heavy bowl ever could.

### **Tips for Getting This Recipe Right**

A handful of details separate a good bowl from a great one.

Rinse your black beans thoroughly before adding them. This removes excess sodium and the starchy liquid they're canned in, which can make the dish taste muddier if left in.

Stir the cottage cheese in while the pan still has some warmth, but not while it's actively boiling. Too much direct heat can cause it to separate slightly instead of melting in smoothly.

Don't skip julienning the peppers. Strips hold up through the simmer better than small dice, which tend to soften into the background instead of standing out.

Use a lean beef blend, but don't go too lean. Something in the 90/10 range still has enough fat to carry flavor, while a 96/4 blend can turn dry and chalky in this dish.

### **Making This a Balanced Meal**

This version already balances itself fairly well between protein, fiber, and complex carbohydrates thanks to the beans and reduced rice. If you want to round it out further, a side of steamed green beans or a simple mixed salad adds extra fiber and volume without much added effort.

Portion size still matters here, even with the added protein. A moderate bowl paired with a side vegetable tends to feel just as satisfying as a heaping plate eaten alone, and it leaves room for the leftovers to actually last through the week.

### **A Note on Sodium**

Between the beef broth, Worcestershire sauce, beans, and three types of dairy, sodium can add up quickly in this dish. Using a low sodium beef broth and rinsing the beans well are the two easiest ways to bring that number down without sacrificing flavor.

If you're managing blood pressure or another condition that requires sodium monitoring, these two swaps make a real difference without changing how the dish tastes.

### **Conclusion**

This high-protein unstuffed peppers recipe takes a comforting, one-pan dinner and quietly makes it work harder for you. Leaner beef, a can of black beans, and a scoop of cottage cheese turn a rice-heavy casserole into a meal that actually holds you over, without asking you to give up any of the flavor that made the original worth making in the first place.

It's not about turning dinner into a meal prep container. It's about making the dinner you were already going to eat do a little more for your body.
