Peach season doesn't last long, and cereal isn't doing your fruit bowl any favors. Peach muffins are the move here. They're soft, genuinely peachy in a way a lot of fruit baked goods promise and don't deliver, and they travel well if you need a grab-and-go breakfast or something to bring to a picnic.

You don't need a reason beyond "it's peach season" to make these. A gingery streusel crumb on top adds crunch, the batter itself is loaded with real peach in two different forms, and the whole thing comes together without much fuss.

### **Why These Peach Muffins Actually Taste Like Peach**

Most peach baked goods lean on peach in name only. A little extract, a few chopped pieces, and the flavor gets lost in all the flour and sugar around it. This recipe avoids that by using peach twice, in two different textures.

Part of the peach gets blended smooth into the wet batter, skin and all. That's not a shortcut. The skin holds a lot of the peach's actual flavor and a beautiful blush of color that plain peeled fruit doesn't have. The rest of the peach gets chopped and folded in at the end, so you get pockets of real fruit in every bite alongside that deep, blended peach flavor running through the whole muffin.

The streusel topping is optional, technically. Realistically, you should make it anyway. A teaspoon of ground ginger in the crumb plays off the peach in a way that tastes like more effort than it actually takes.

#### **What You'll Need**

This recipe comes together in two parts: a streusel topping and the muffin batter itself. Make the streusel first since it needs a few minutes to come together into crumbly little nuggets.

##### **For the streusel topping:**

* 45 grams (about 1/3 cup) plain flour
* 2 tablespoons Demerara sugar
* A good pinch of salt
* 1 teaspoon ground ginger
* 2 tablespoons melted butter

##### **For the muffins:**

* 240 grams (about 2 cups) plain flour
* 2 1/2 teaspoons baking powder
* 120 grams (about 1/2 cup plus 2 tablespoons) caster sugar
* 60 grams (about 1/4 cup) light soft brown sugar
* A good pinch of salt
* 106 grams ripe peach, unpeeled (roughly one small peach)
* 190 grams (about 3/4 cup) plain yogurt
* 2 large eggs
* 70 grams (about 1/3 cup) sunflower oil
* 70 grams (about 1/3 cup) melted butter
* A drop of almond extract, optional
* 140 grams ripe peach, chopped (roughly one medium peach)
* Optional: extra peach slices for topping

You'll also need a 12-hole muffin pan lined with muffin or cupcake cases, and a high-speed blender for the peach and wet ingredients.

### **How to Make Peach Muffins**

#### **Step 1: Make the streusel topping**.

In a small bowl, combine the flour, Demerara sugar, salt, and ground ginger. Stir with a fork until evenly mixed. Add the melted butter and mix again with the fork, which breaks everything up and combines it at the same time. If the mixture feels too wet, add a touch more flour. If it feels too dry, add a bit more melted butter or a drop of water. You're aiming for crumbly little nuggets, not a paste. Set this aside.

#### **Step 2: Mix the dry ingredients.**

In a large bowl, sieve together the flour, baking powder, caster sugar, brown sugar, and salt. Sieving matters more than it sounds like it should here. Brown sugar in particular tends to clump, and pushing everything through a sieve breaks that up while mixing the ingredients evenly at the same time.

#### **Step 3: Blend the wet ingredients.**

In a high-speed blender, combine the 106 grams of peach (skin on), plain yogurt, eggs, sunflower oil, melted butter, and almond extract if using. Blend until completely smooth. Leaving the peach skin on isn't just about convenience. It adds color and real flavor that peeled fruit doesn't carry.

#### **Step 4: Combine wet and dry.**

Pour the blended wet mixture over the dry ingredients. Stir everything together with a fork rather than a whisk or mixer. This matters. Overworking muffin batter develops the gluten in the flour, and that's what turns muffins dense and tough instead of light. Stop stirring while you can still see a little flour in the bowl.

#### **Step 5: Fold in the chopped peach.**

Add the 140 grams of chopped peach and fold it in briefly, just enough to distribute it through the batter. Then transfer the mixture to a jug for easier pouring into your muffin cases.

#### **Step 6: Fill the muffin cases.**

Fill each lined muffin hole generously, up to about half a centimeter from the top. The batter will be quite runny, and that's expected. These muffins rise well, so don't be shy about filling them close to the top.

#### **Step 7: Add your toppings.**

Sprinkle streusel over some muffins, add a slice of peach to others, or do both together. If you have leftover streusel, don't toss it. Freeze it and use it later on crumbles or another batch of muffins.

#### **Step 8: Bake.**

Bake at 160°C (320°F) in a fan oven, adjusting slightly higher if your oven doesn't have a fan setting, for 35 to 40 minutes. Start checking at the 35 minute mark with a toothpick inserted into the center. You're looking for moist crumbs clinging to the toothpick, not wet batter.

#### **Step 9: Cool and enjoy.**

Let the muffins cool in the pan for a few minutes before moving them to a wire rack. They'll smell like ginger and warm peaches, and it's genuinely hard to wait for them to cool all the way before grabbing one.

### **Tips for Getting These Right**

The batter really is meant to be runny. If it looks thinner than a typical muffin batter you've made before, that's not a mistake. It's what keeps these soft and bouncy instead of dry.

Ripeness matters more than most people expect. You want peaches that give slightly under a gentle squeeze but don't fall apart. Underripe peaches won't blend smoothly, and overripe ones can throw off the batter's texture. If your peaches aren't quite there yet, leave them on the counter for a day or two before baking.

Don't skip the sieving step for the dry ingredients, even if it feels like an extra dish to wash. Brown sugar clumps more than people realize, and a few nuggety bits of sugar in a bite of muffin isn't pleasant.

If almond extract isn't something you keep around, leaving it out is completely fine. It's there to boost the peach flavor slightly, not to carry the recipe.

### **Storage and Make-Ahead Tips**

These muffins keep well at room temperature in an airtight container for about two days. After that, the streusel topping starts to soften, so the fridge is a better call if you're planning to eat them over a longer stretch, up to about five days.

For longer storage, these freeze nicely. Wrap cooled muffins individually and freeze them for up to two months. Pull one out the night before or give it a quick warm-up in the microwave for a fast breakfast that still tastes freshly baked.

If you have extra streusel left over after topping your muffins, freeze it in a small bag. It keeps well and is worth having on hand for a second batch or a quick fruit crumble later in the season.

### **Conclusion**

Peach muffins aren't a complicated bake, but they deliver a lot for the effort involved. Real peach in two textures, a gingery crumb on top, and a soft, bouncy crumb underneath make this one worth adding to your peach season rotation.

Make a batch while peaches are actually good. Bring some to a picnic, freeze a few for later, and don't skip the streusel even though technically you could.
