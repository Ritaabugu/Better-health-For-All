You will not taste the zucchini. That's not a sales pitch, it's just how this cake works. The grated zucchini melts into the batter and leaves behind moisture, not flavor, which is exactly why this cake turns out as soft as it does.

If you've ever made carrot cake and loved that combination of warm spice and tangy cream cheese frosting, this zucchini cake lives in the same family. Cinnamon, a little nutmeg, chopped walnuts for crunch, and a frosting that isn't trying too hard to be sweet. It's the kind of cake that gets requested again once people find out what's actually in it.

### **Zucchini Cake: A Must-Try Recipe This Season**

This recipe makes one 9 by 13 inch cake, frosted and ready to slice. Total time runs about an hour, most of it hands off while the cake bakes and cools.

#### **Ingredients**

**For the cake**

* 210g (1 3/4 cups) all-purpose flour
* 30g (1/4 cup) cornstarch
* 3/4 teaspoon baking soda
* 1 1/2 teaspoons baking powder
* 1/2 teaspoon salt
* 2 1/2 teaspoons ground cinnamon
* 1/4 teaspoon ground nutmeg
* 300g (1 1/2 cups) soft light brown sugar
* 2 large eggs, room temperature
* 120g (1/2 cup) whole milk, room temperature
* 160g (3/4 cup) unflavored vegetable oil (canola works well)
* 2 1/2 teaspoons vanilla extract
* 190g (1 1/2 cups) freshly grated zucchini, not blotted dry
* 100g (1 cup) chopped walnuts (pecans work too, if you prefer)

##### **For the cream cheese frosting**

* 112g (1/2 cup) unsalted butter, room temperature
* 215g (1 3/4 cups) powdered sugar
* 167g (3/4 cup) cold cream cheese (firm, not the spreadable kind)
* 3/4 teaspoon vanilla extract
* 1 teaspoon fresh lemon juice

#### **Instructions**

**Prep the pan and oven**

* Preheat the oven to 350°F (180°C) conventional.
* Lightly grease a 9 by 13 inch cake pan.
* Line the pan lengthwise with parchment paper, leaving some overhang on the sides. This makes lifting the finished cake out significantly easier, so don't skip it.

##### **Mix the dry ingredients**

* In a bowl, sift together the flour, cornstarch, baking soda, baking powder, salt, cinnamon, and nutmeg.
* Whisk until well combined, then set the bowl aside.

#### **Mix the wet ingredients**

* In a large bowl, whisk together the brown sugar, eggs, milk, vegetable oil, and vanilla.
* Add the freshly grated zucchini directly into the wet mixture. There's no need to blot it dry first, the moisture is doing real work in this recipe.
* Whisk until everything is well combined.

#### **Bring the batter together**

* Add the dry ingredients to the wet ingredients.
* Using a spatula, gently fold until just combined. Stop as soon as the flour streaks disappear. Overmixing at this stage is the most common way to end up with a denser cake than you wanted.
* Fold in the chopped walnuts, gently, until just distributed through the batter.

#### **Bake**

* Pour the batter into the prepared pan. Give the pan a light shake to even out the surface, then drop it once on the counter to release any large air bubbles.
* Bake for 25 minutes, or until a toothpick inserted into the center comes out clean with just a few moist crumbs clinging to it.
* Let the cake cool in the pan for 20 minutes. This gives it time to firm up before you handle it, and this cake is soft enough that skipping this step risks breaking it.
* Run a thin knife along the edges of the pan that don't have parchment paper, then turn the cake out onto a wire rack. Placing the rack on top of the pan, holding both together, and flipping in one motion works well here.
* Let the cake cool completely on the rack before frosting it.

#### **Make the cream cheese frosting**

* Add the room temperature butter to a large bowl. Using a hand or stand mixer on medium speed, cream it for a few minutes until light and fluffy. If you're using a stand mixer, the paddle attachment is the right tool here.
* Add the powdered sugar in two batches, mixing on low speed each time so it doesn't end up airborne. Make sure each batch is fully incorporated before adding the next.
* Add the cold cream cheese, vanilla, and lemon juice. Mix until smooth and well combined.
* Stop as soon as it comes together. Overmixing the frosting at this stage actually makes it softer, not fluffier, which isn't what you want when you're about to spread it.

#### **Assemble the cake**

* Once the cake has cooled completely, place your serving plate or tray over it, then flip the cake and rack together so the cake lands on the plate right side up.
* Spread the frosting evenly over the top using an offset spatula, a blunt knife, or the back of a spoon, whichever you have on hand.
* Slice and serve.

### **A Few Notes That Actually Matter**

Don't blot the zucchini dry before adding it to the batter. This trips people up because most zucchini recipes tell you to squeeze out the water first. Here, that moisture is exactly what keeps the cake as soft as it is.

Room temperature ingredients matter more than they get credit for in a recipe like this. Cold eggs and milk don't incorporate as smoothly into the batter, and cold butter won't cream properly for the frosting. Pull everything out about 30 minutes before you start.

The cream cheese for the frosting is the one exception. Keep it cold. Firm, cold cream cheese holds its structure better than the soft spreadable kind, and it keeps the frosting from turning runny once it hits the warm cake.

If you're worried about overmixing, watch for streaks instead of counting strokes. Fold the batter just until you can't see dry flour anymore, and stop there. The same goes for the walnuts.

### **Storage and Make-Ahead Tips**

This cake keeps well, which makes it a solid choice if you're baking for a gathering and want to get ahead of it. Store the frosted cake in the fridge, covered, for up to four days. Let slices sit at room temperature for about 15 minutes before serving so the frosting softens back up.

You can also bake the cake a day ahead and frost it the next day. Wrap the unfrosted, cooled cake tightly and keep it at room temperature overnight, then frost it right before serving.

### **Why This One Works So Well**

Zucchini cakes can go one of two ways. Either the zucchini gets lost entirely and you end up with a dry, forgettable cake, or it's so present that people can taste exactly what they're eating and get weird about it. This recipe lands in the middle. The cornstarch alongside the flour keeps the crumb tender, the spice blend gives it warmth without tasting like pumpkin bread's cousin, and the walnuts add just enough texture that every bite isn't uniformly soft.

The frosting deserves some credit too. It's not oversweetened, and the lemon juice cuts through the richness just enough to keep you reaching for a second slice instead of feeling like you need a break after the first.

### **A Quick Health Note**

This is a dessert, and it's meant to be enjoyed as one. It does include a serving of vegetables in the form of grated zucchini, along with some fiber from the walnuts, but the sugar and butter content mean it's still a treat rather than a health food. If you're managing blood sugar or another condition where dietary sugar and fat need close attention, it's worth talking to your doctor or a registered dietitian about how a recipe like this fits into your routine.

### **Conclusion**

This zucchini cake earns its spot as a seasonal favorite honestly. It's soft in a way that's hard to get from a boxed mix, it uses up zucchini before it goes bad in your fridge, and the cream cheese frosting on top makes it feel like the kind of cake you'd actually order at a bakery.

Make it once and you'll understand why people keep coming back to zucchini cake even though it sounds like an odd combination on paper. Grate the zucchini, don't blot it, fold gently, and let the oven do the rest. That's really all there is to it.
