If your garden has handed you more zucchini than you know what to do with, this is the recipe that finally makes a dent in the pile. Gluten free zucchini bread sounds like it should be a compromise, dry, dense, a little sad compared to the "real" thing. This one isn't. It's wet, tender, and genuinely moist in a way that surprises people who expect gluten-free baking to fall short.

The whole thing comes together in about ten minutes of prep. No mixer, no complicated technique, just a bowl, a whisk, and a loaf pan. And if you need it dairy-free too, one simple ingredient swap gets you there without changing the texture at all.

### **Why This Zucchini Bread Actually Works**

Most quick breads aren't anyone's favorite. They tend to be fine. A little dry, a little forgettable, the kind of thing you eat because it's there. This one is different, and the reason comes down to a few small choices that make a real difference.

First, the zucchini goes in unpeeled and unsqueezed. A lot of recipes tell you to wring out every drop of water before adding shredded zucchini, and that instinct makes sense on paper. In practice, it just dries out the loaf. Leaving the water in is what gives this bread its signature moistness, and skipping that step is honestly the whole secret.

Second, the batter itself is loose and runny, wetter than you'd expect from a standard quick bread. That extra liquid is doing real work. It's part of why the finished loaf stays tender for days instead of turning stale and crumbly by day two.

And third, this recipe is genuinely flexible. Swap one ingredient in the flour blend and it's dairy-free. Skip that swap and it's just gluten-free. Either way, nobody eating it will be able to tell the difference from a regular loaf of zucchini bread, which is really the whole point.

### **What You'll Need**

This recipe uses a gluten-free all-purpose flour blend rather than a single flour, which is what gives the texture its structure. Here's everything you'll need.

* 1 1/2 cups plus 2 tablespoons (227 g) gluten-free all-purpose flour blend
* 1/2 cup (100 g) brown sugar
* 1/2 cup (100 g) granulated sugar
* 2 teaspoons baking soda
* 3/4 teaspoon salt
* 1/2 teaspoon ground nutmeg
* 1/2 teaspoon ground cinnamon
* 1 cup shredded zucchini, unpeeled, from about one medium zucchini
* 2 large eggs
* 1/2 cup (120 ml) canola oil (vegetable oil or melted coconut oil both work)
* 1/3 cup plus 1 tablespoon (92 ml) water
* 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
* Optional topping: 1 tablespoon sugar mixed with 1/2 teaspoon ground cinnamon

If you need this loaf dairy-free, check the flour blend you're using. Many gluten-free all-purpose blends include dry milk powder for structure. Swapping that for coconut milk powder makes the whole recipe dairy-free without changing how it bakes or tastes. If dairy isn't a concern for you, the regular blend with dry milk powder works just fine.

### **How to Make Gluten Free Zucchini Bread**

#### **Step 1: Preheat and prep your pan.**

Preheat your oven to 350°F. Spray an 8 1/2 by 4 1/2 inch loaf pan with non-stick spray, then line it with a sheet of parchment paper that overhangs the edges slightly. This makes lifting the finished loaf out of the pan far easier, and it's worth the extra thirty seconds.

#### **Step 2: Whisk the dry ingredients.**

In a large bowl, whisk together the flour blend, brown sugar, granulated sugar, baking soda, salt, nutmeg, and cinnamon. Make sure everything is evenly combined before moving on.

#### **Step 3: Add the zucchini and eggs.**

Add your shredded zucchini directly to the dry ingredients, water and all. Crack in the eggs on top.

#### **Step 4: Add the wet ingredients and whisk.**

Pour in the oil, water, and vanilla extract. Take a whisk and break up the eggs first, then whisk everything together until fully combined. The batter will be noticeably wet and runny. That's not a mistake. It's exactly what makes this loaf so moist.

#### **Step 5: Pour and bake.**

Pour the batter into your prepared pan and smooth the top. If you want a little crunch, sprinkle the cinnamon sugar topping over the batter now. Bake for 50 to 60 minutes, or until a toothpick inserted into the center comes out clean.

#### **Step 6: Cool before slicing.**

Let the loaf cool in the pan for about 10 to 15 minutes, then use the parchment overhang to lift it out onto a wire rack. Resist cutting into it too early. Quick breads finish setting as they cool, and a warm loaf is much harder to slice cleanly.

That's genuinely all there is to it. No stand mixer, no resting time, no complicated technique to get wrong.

### **Tips, Swaps, and Variations**

This recipe holds up well to changes, which makes it a good one to keep in your regular rotation instead of a one-time bake.

If your zucchini harvest is bigger than one loaf can handle, double or even triple the recipe and freeze extra slices. Wrapped well, they hold their texture nicely and thaw in about twenty minutes on the counter. You can also turn the batter into muffins instead of a loaf if you want individual portions that freeze even more easily.

Mix-ins work well here too. Chopped walnuts or pecans add crunch and a little more staying power if you're eating this as a snack rather than dessert. Chocolate chips turn it into something a lot closer to cake, which isn't a bad thing depending on what you're going for.

As for the oil, canola and vegetable oil behave almost identically here. Melted coconut oil adds a faint coconut note that pairs nicely with the cinnamon and nutmeg, so it's worth trying if you already keep it on hand.

### **How to Store It**

Once the loaf has cooled completely, store it at room temperature in an airtight container for up to three days, or in the fridge for about a week. If you're planning to keep it longer, freezing individual slices is the better move. Wrap each slice tightly and pull them out one at a time as you want them.

This bread also tends to taste even better on day two, once the flavors have had a chance to settle and the texture firms up slightly from that very wet batter.

### **Conclusion**

Gluten free zucchini bread has a reputation it doesn't deserve. Done right, it's not a consolation prize for people avoiding gluten. It's just a good loaf of zucchini bread that happens to work for more people at the table.

If you've got zucchini piling up and ten minutes to spare, this is worth making this week. Double the batch if your garden is being particularly generous, and don't be afraid to leave the water in the zucchini. That's the part doing all the work.
