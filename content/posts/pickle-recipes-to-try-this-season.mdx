You've got a farmers market haul sitting on your counter and no real plan for half of it. Pickle recipes are the answer. No canning equipment required, no all-day process, just jars, brine, and a little patience in the fridge.

These are quick pickles, meaning everything stays refrigerated instead of shelf-stable. They're also, unexpectedly, one of the easiest ways to get more vegetables into your week. Once a jar of pickled carrots or green beans is sitting in the fridge, people reach for them the same way they'd reach for chips.

### **Pickle Recipes Worth Making This Summer**

Every recipe below uses the same basic idea. Pack your produce into a clean jar, pour a hot brine over it, seal it, and refrigerate. The brine is almost always some combination of vinegar, water, salt, and sometimes sugar. Once you understand that formula, you can riff on nearly any vegetable in your fridge.

A few of these use the exact same brine for two different vegetables, which makes prep faster if you're pickling several things in one sitting. Save any old glass jars for this. Pasta sauce jars, salsa jars, whatever you've got works fine.

#### **1. Classic Garlic Dill Pickles**

This is the pickle recipe most people picture when they hear the word. It's crunchy, garlicky, and genuinely better than anything you'll find at the store.

##### **Ingredients**:

* Pickling cucumbers (sometimes called salad cucumbers), sliced
* Whole peppercorns
* Fresh garlic cloves
* Fresh dill
* 8 cups water
* 1½ cups vinegar
* 6 tablespoons kosher salt

##### **Instructions**:

1. Wash and slice your cucumbers.
2. Sprinkle peppercorns into a clean jar, then add garlic cloves and fresh dill.
3. Pack the cucumber slices in tightly. The tighter the pack, the less room for air pockets.
4. Combine the water, vinegar, and kosher salt in a pot. Heat and stir occasionally until the salt fully dissolves. It doesn't need to reach a rolling boil.
5. Let the brine cool slightly, then pour it into the jar all the way to the top.
6. Seal and refrigerate.

#### **2. Pickled Carrots**

Carrots hold their crunch beautifully in a quick pickle, which makes them a great snacking option straight from the jar.

##### **Ingredients**:

* Carrots, peeled and cut into thin sticks
* 1 part water
* 1 part vinegar
* ¼ cup kosher salt
* ½ cup sugar

##### **Instructions**:

1. Peel your carrots and slice them into thin sticks. Thinner sticks let the brine soak all the way through while keeping some crunch.
2. Pack the carrot sticks into a jar.
3. Combine equal parts water and vinegar with the salt and sugar in a pot. Heat until fully dissolved and clear.
4. Let the brine cool slightly, then pour it over the carrots while still warm.
5. Seal and refrigerate. Give it at least 12 to 24 hours before eating for the best flavor, though a couple of hours will still work in a pinch.

#### **3. Pickled Jalapenos**

This uses the same brine as the pickled carrots, so make a double batch if you're doing both at once.

##### **Ingredients**:

* Jalapenos, thinly sliced (seeds left in for more heat)
* 1 part water
* 1 part vinegar
* ¼ cup kosher salt
* ½ cup sugar

##### **Instructions**:

1. Slice your jalapenos thin. Because they're potent, thinner slices distribute the heat more evenly than thick ones.
2. Leave the seeds in for a spicier jar, or choose fatter, rounder jalapenos for a milder result.
3. Pack the slices into a jar.
4. Use the same brine as the pickled carrots above. Pour it over the peppers while hot, which helps soften them slightly.
5. Seal and let cool to room temperature before refrigerating.

#### **4. Sweet Banana Peppers**

Not every jar needs heat. These banana peppers are sweet, mild, and the kind of thing you eat straight from the jar with a fork.

##### **Ingredients**:

* Banana peppers
* Optional: a clove of garlic, a slice of jalapeno
* 1⅓ cups water
* 2 cups vinegar
* A heaping teaspoon of salt

##### **Instructions**:

1. Pack whole or sliced banana peppers into a jar. Add a clove of garlic or a slice of jalapeno if you want extra flavor.
2. Combine the water, vinegar, and salt in a pot and bring to a boil.
3. Pour the hot brine directly over the peppers.
4. Let the jar cool to room temperature, then refrigerate.

#### **5. Pickled Okra**

Okra pickles beautifully, and this version is built for one very specific purpose: garnishing a proper Bloody Mary.

##### **Ingredients**:

* Okra, ends trimmed
* Fresh dill
* Garlic cloves
* Dried chilies
* 4 cups water
* 3 cups vinegar
* Kosher salt, to taste

##### **Instructions**:

1. Wash your okra and trim off the brown ends.
2. Pack the okra into a jar, alternating direction so it fits snugly.
3. Add fresh dill, garlic cloves, and dried chilies. The chilies don't need to be reconstituted first. The hot brine will soften them as it's poured in.
4. Combine the water, vinegar, and salt in a pot and bring to a boil.
5. Pour the hot brine over the okra, seal, and refrigerate.

#### **6. Pickled Green Beans**

This uses the same base brine as the pickled okra, just seasoned a little differently.

##### **Ingredients**:

* Green beans, washed and trimmed
* Fresh dill
* Garlic cloves
* Whole peppercorns
* 4 cups water
* 3 cups vinegar
* Kosher salt, to taste

##### **Instructions**:

1. Stack the trimmed green beans into a jar.
2. Add fresh dill, garlic cloves, and peppercorns.
3. Combine the water, vinegar, and salt in a pot and bring to a boil.
4. Pour the brine over the green beans carefully. It's easy to spill this part, so take your time.
5. Seal and refrigerate.

#### **7. Pickled Shrimp**

This one is a genuine summer specialty in the South, and it's the pickle recipe on this list most likely to steal the show at a party.

##### **Ingredients**:

* Shrimp, peeled and deveined, tails left on
* Old Bay seasoning
* 1 red onion, thinly sliced into rings
* 1 Fresno chili, thinly sliced
* 1 lemon, thinly sliced
* 3 garlic cloves, smashed
* Bay leaves
* Capers, roughly chopped
* Celery seed
* Lemon juice
* 1 cup apple cider vinegar
* ½ cup olive oil

##### **Instructions**:

1. Bring a pot of heavily seasoned water to a boil, using Old Bay for flavor.
2. While the water heats, build your pickling mixture in a bowl. Combine the sliced red onion, Fresno chili, lemon slices, smashed garlic, bay leaves, chopped capers, and celery seed.
3. Add the lemon juice, apple cider vinegar, and olive oil to the bowl. Stir well and let it sit while you cook the shrimp.
4. Add the shrimp to the boiling water and cook for exactly 2 minutes. Set a timer so you don't overcook them.
5. Drain the shrimp immediately into the pickling mixture. The cool liquid stops the cooking process right away.
6. Cover and refrigerate. Be patient here. The flavor builds the longer it sits.

#### **8. Pickled Red Onions**

If there's one jar on this list you should always have stocked, it's this one. It takes minutes and works on nearly everything from tacos to sandwiches.

##### **Ingredients**:

* Red onion, thinly sliced
* Vinegar
* Salt
* Sugar

##### **Instructions**:

1. Slice your red onion thin and pack it into a jar.
2. Unlike most of the other recipes here, this brine skips water entirely. Combine just vinegar, salt, and sugar in a pot.
3. Heat until it reaches a simmer and everything is fully dissolved.
4. Pour the hot brine directly over the sliced onions.
5. Seal and refrigerate. This one has a short wait time, so you can enjoy it within a few hours.

#### **9. Pickled Garlic**

This is a quieter recipe, but it's worth keeping in your back pocket year round, not just during peak produce season.

##### **Ingredients**:

* Garlic cloves (halved if very large)
* Bell pepper, sliced
* 2 cups plain white vinegar
* ⅔ cup sugar
* Celery seed
* A pinch of dried mustard

##### **Instructions**:

1. Pack garlic cloves and bell pepper slices into a jar, alternating between the two.
2. Combine the vinegar, sugar, celery seed, and dried mustard in a pot and bring to a boil.
3. Pour the hot brine over the garlic and peppers while it's still very hot.
4. Cover and let the jar sit briefly before refrigerating, which lets everything continue softening.

#### **10. Pickled Zucchini**

This is the one most likely to surprise you. The spongy inside of a zucchini soaks up brine differently than a cucumber does, and the payoff is worth the extra step.

##### **Ingredients**:

* Zucchini, sliced
* 1 onion, sliced
* Salt (for the initial soak)
* 2 cups apple cider vinegar
* 2 cups sugar
* Celery seed
* Mustard seed
* Turmeric
* Yellow mustard

##### **Instructions**:

1. Soak the sliced zucchini and onion in water with salt for a couple of hours. This step is worth the wait, so don't skip it.
2. While that soaks, combine the apple cider vinegar, sugar, celery seed, mustard seed, turmeric, and yellow mustard in a pot.
3. Bring the mixture to a boil and cook until the sugar fully dissolves.
4. Drain the salt water from the zucchini and onion, then add them directly into the pot with the hot brine. Stir and turn off the heat.
5. Transfer everything to jars, packing tightly, or let it sit in the pot briefly first.
6. Refrigerate. The color turns a striking yellow thanks to the turmeric, and the flavor is unlike a standard cucumber pickle.

### **A Few Tips Before You Start**

Quick pickles like these are forgiving, but a few small habits make a real difference. Pack your jars tightly. Once the produce is in, liquid is all that's left to fill the gaps, so a loose pack means uneven pickling.

Pour hot brine carefully. Nearly every one of these recipes involves pouring a hot liquid into a jar, and it spills more easily than you'd expect. Take your time here.

Patience matters more than most people assume. A few of these can technically be eaten within a couple of hours, but flavor develops significantly over the first 12 to 24 hours. If you can wait, wait.

### **Conclusion**

These pickle recipes cover a lot of ground, from classic garlic dill pickles to pickled shrimp built for a Bloody Mary bar. What ties them together is how little effort each one takes for how much flavor comes back.

Grab whatever's sitting in your produce drawer, pick one of these pickle recipes, and give it a try this week. Once you taste the difference between homemade and store shelf, it's hard to go back.
