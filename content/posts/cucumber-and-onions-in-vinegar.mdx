Some recipes don't need a headline act. Cucumber and onions in vinegar is one of them. It's the side dish that shows up, does its job, and quietly steals the plate.

No stove. No oven. Just a knife, a bowl, and about fifteen minutes between you and something crisp, tangy, and genuinely refreshing. If summer had a flavor, this would be it.

### **Why This Recipe Works**

You don't need a reason to love a recipe this simple, but here's one anyway: it's forgiving. There's no precise temperature to hit, no timing window you have to nail. You slice, you whisk, you toss. Done.

It also solves a problem a lot of us have in the summer heat: nobody wants to turn on the oven when it's ninety degrees outside. This dish doesn't ask you to. It just asks for a cutting board and a little patience while the flavors settle in.

And the flavor itself earns its keep. The vinegar brings sharpness. The sugar rounds it out just enough that it never tastes harsh. The dill adds something herby and cool. Bite into a cucumber slice that's been sitting in that dressing for an hour and you'll understand why this recipe has survived generations of backyard cookouts without needing a single update.

### **What You'll Need**

This recipe uses ingredients most people already have sitting in the fridge or pantry. That's part of the appeal. No specialty store run required.

#### **For the cucumbers and onions:**

* 2 English cucumbers, thinly sliced
* 1 cup red onion, thinly sliced (or sweet yellow onion, if that's what you've got)

#### **For the vinegar dressing:**

* ⅓ cup apple cider vinegar
* ¼ cup water
* 1 tablespoon sugar
* 1 teaspoon dried dill (or fresh, if you have it on hand)
* A couple pinches of salt
* A couple pinches of black pepper

That's the whole list. Eight ingredients, and half of them are already in your spice rack.

#### **A Quick Note on Cucumbers**

English cucumbers are the move here, and not just because they taste good. Their skin is thin enough that you don't have to peel them, which means less prep and less waste. If you've got homegrown cucumbers from a garden or a farmers market, those work beautifully too. Just give them a good rinse first.

If English cucumbers aren't available, regular cucumbers will do the job. You'll just want to peel them first, since the skin tends to be tougher and slightly bitter.

### **How to Make Cucumber and Onions in Vinegar**

Here's where the actual cooking, or rather, the actual not-cooking, happens.

#### **Step 1: Slice the Cucumbers**

Wash your cucumbers and slice them thin. A mandoline makes quick work of this and keeps the slices even, which matters more than you'd think. Even slices soak up the dressing at the same rate, so every bite tastes the same. Once you're done, pour the slices into a large mixing bowl.

#### **Step 2: Slice the Onion**

Take your red onion and slice it thin too. You're not using the whole onion here. About a cup is plenty. Separate the layers as you go so they mix evenly with the cucumbers instead of clumping into onion rings nobody asked for. Add the sliced onion to the bowl with your cucumbers.

#### **Step 3: Whisk the Dressing**

In a separate small bowl or measuring cup, combine the apple cider vinegar, water, sugar, dill, salt, and pepper. Whisk it until the sugar fully dissolves. This part takes less than a minute, but don't rush it. Undissolved sugar means an uneven dressing, and an uneven dressing means some bites taste sweeter than others.

#### **Step 4: Combine and Toss**

Pour the dressing over your cucumbers and onions. Toss everything together until it's evenly coated. That's genuinely it. No baking, no simmering, no waiting for anything to reduce.

#### **Step 5: Let It Sit**

This is the step people skip, and it's the one that matters most. The dish tastes good right away, but it tastes better after it's had time to marinate. Let it sit in the fridge for at least thirty minutes if you're short on time. An hour is better. Overnight is best, if you can manage the patience.

The longer it sits, the more the cucumbers pull in that vinegar and dill flavor, and the more the onion mellows out. What starts sharp turns into something rounded and balanced.

#### **Tips for Making It Your Own**

This recipe is a solid foundation, not a locked formula. A few ways to adjust it based on what you've got or what you like:

Swap the apple cider vinegar for white vinegar or rice vinegar if that's what's in your pantry. Each brings a slightly different edge, but the base idea holds up.

Try fresh dill instead of dried if you have it growing or can grab a bunch at the store. Fresh herbs tend to taste brighter, though dried works perfectly well when that's what's on hand.

Add a pinch of red pepper flakes if you want a little heat mixed into that cool, tangy flavor. It's not traditional, but it's not wrong either.

Use a sweet onion instead of red if you find red onion too sharp on its own. Sweet onions bring a gentler flavor that some people prefer, especially kids who are picky about raw onion.

#### **Serving Ideas**

This dish doesn't need much of an introduction at the table. It pairs well with grilled chicken, burgers, or really anything coming off a summer grill. It also works as a light lunch on its own, especially on a day when the last thing you want is a hot meal.

Some people like to spoon a little of the extra vinegar dressing over the top right before serving, since it tends to settle at the bottom of the bowl. Others skip that step and let the cucumbers speak for themselves. Both approaches work.

Leftovers keep well in the fridge for several days, and honestly, they might taste even better on day two.

#### **Storage**

Keep any leftovers in an airtight container in the refrigerator. The cucumbers will continue to soften the longer they sit in the vinegar, so if you like a firmer bite, plan to eat this within the first day or two. If you don't mind a softer texture, it'll still taste good closer to the end of the week.

This isn't a recipe meant for freezing. The texture just doesn't hold up once it thaws, so make only what you'll eat within a few days.

### **A Simple Dish Worth Keeping Around**

There's something worth noticing about a recipe that's stuck around this long without needing much change. Cucumber and onions in vinegar isn't flashy. It doesn't require a special occasion or a complicated technique. It just works, the way a handful of really good, really simple recipes tend to.

If you're looking for something light to bring to a cookout, something to pack for lunch, or just a way to use up cucumbers before they go soft in the crisper drawer, this is worth keeping in the rotation. Slice, whisk, toss, wait. That's the whole process, and it delivers every single time.
