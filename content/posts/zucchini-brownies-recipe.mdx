There's a point every summer where the zucchini stops being a vegetable and starts being a situation. One plant produces more than any household can reasonably eat, and by August you're either giving it away at work or figuring out what else it can become. Zucchini brownies are the answer nobody expects and everybody remembers.

No eggs, no complicated technique, and a texture that regular brownies genuinely can't match. The zucchini isn't a gimmick here. It's doing real work, and once you understand what that work is, you'll want an excuse to make these again before the season's over.

### **Why Zucchini Belongs in Brownies**

Here's the part that surprises people. You don't peel the zucchini, and you don't squeeze the moisture out of it before mixing it in. Both of those instincts feel right if you've cooked with zucchini before, and both of them would ruin this recipe.

That moisture is the entire point. As the brownies bake, the water inside the shredded zucchini releases into the batter, which is exactly what keeps these brownies from drying out. Regular brownies rely on eggs and butter for moisture and structure. This version skips the eggs entirely and lets the zucchini do that job instead. The result is a brownie that's noticeably denser and moister than a boxed mix, with a texture closer to fudge than cake.

### **What You'll Need**

##### **Dry ingredients:**

* 2 cups all-purpose flour
* 1 ½ teaspoons baking soda
* 1 teaspoon salt
* ½ cup Dutch baking cocoa (a dark cocoa works well if you like a deeper chocolate flavor)
* 1 ½ cups sugar

##### **Wet ingredients:**

* ½ cup oil (vegetable, canola, or peanut oil all work)
* 2 teaspoons vanilla extract
* 2 cups shredded zucchini, unpeeled
* Up to 5 tablespoons water, added gradually as needed

##### **Mix-ins:**

* 1 cup walnuts or pecans, or leave the nuts out entirely

##### **Equipment**:

* A large mixing bowl (this batter is thick, so don't underestimate the bowl size you need)
* A box grater or shredder
* A 9x13 baking pan
* Parchment paper

### **How to Make Zucchini Brownies**

#### **Step 1: Prep Your Zucchini**

Shred about 2 cups of zucchini. Don't peel it first. The skin adds texture and nutrients, and there's no real reason to remove it. Don't press or squeeze out the moisture either. That water is what turns these into the moistest brownies you'll make this year.

#### **Step 2: Mix the Dry Ingredients**

In a large bowl, combine the flour, baking soda, salt, cocoa, and sugar. Stir until evenly mixed. Use a bigger bowl than you think you need. This batter gets thick fast, and a small bowl makes the next steps messier than they need to be.

#### **Step 3: Add the Wet Ingredients**

Pour in the oil and vanilla, then add the shredded zucchini. Start stirring. The mixture will look dry and almost powdery at first, closer to crumbly dough than batter. That's normal.

#### **Step 4: Add Water Gradually**

Add the water one tablespoon at a time, stirring between each addition. You're looking for the batter to come together into a thick, cookie-dough-like consistency. Depending on how much moisture your zucchini released, you may need the full 5 tablespoons, or you may need less. Keep stirring. It looks like it won't come together, and then it does.

#### **Step 5: Fold in the Nuts**

Stir in the walnuts or pecans if you're using them. The batter will be thick enough that you could technically shape it into cookies instead, though the pan method is easier for a full batch.

#### **Step 6: Prep the Pan and Spread the Batter**

Line a 9x13 baking pan with parchment paper, then lightly spray it or rub it with butter so the brownies lift out cleanly once baked. Spread the batter evenly into the pan. It's thick, so take your time working it into the corners.

#### **Step 7: Bake**

Bake at 350°F. Start checking around 20 minutes, though depending on your oven, it may take closer to 30. You're looking for the top to look set, with the texture of a proper brownie rather than raw batter underneath.

#### **Step 8: Cool and Cut**

Let the brownies cool completely in the pan before lifting them out using the parchment paper. Cutting into clean squares is much easier once they've cooled all the way, even though the smell coming from the kitchen will make that wait feel unreasonable.

### **Tips for Getting the Texture Right**

**Leave the moisture in.** This is the one rule that matters most. Every instinct you have about draining vegetables before baking with them should be ignored here.

**Don't over-shred the zucchini**. A regular box grater setting is fine. You don't need it finely minced, and larger shreds actually help with texture.

**Watch the batter, not just the clock**. Ovens vary enough that bake time can shift by 10 minutes or more. Rely on how the top looks and feels rather than a strict timer.

**Skip the frosting if you can**. These brownies are rich enough on their own that most people don't miss the icing. If you want one anyway, a simple chocolate glaze or cream cheese frosting both work well.

### **Storage**

Once fully cooled, store zucchini brownies in an airtight container at room temperature for up to 3 days, or in the fridge for about a week. The zucchini's moisture means these brownies won't dry out the way a standard batch might, but they also won't last quite as long on the counter before needing refrigeration.

### **A Few Ways to Change This Up**

**Nut-free**. Leave the walnuts or pecans out entirely. The brownies hold together fine without them and stay just as moist.

**Darker chocolate**. A dark Dutch cocoa gives these a deeper, slightly more bitter chocolate flavor that pairs well with the natural sweetness of the zucchini.

**Frozen zucchini works too**. If you've got shredded zucchini frozen from last season, it's a good use for it. Let it thaw and drain slightly less aggressively than you would for other recipes, since that moisture is exactly what you want here.

**Cookie version**. Because the batter is thick enough to hold its shape, you can portion it onto a baking sheet instead of a pan if you'd rather have individual zucchini brownie cookies.

### **Conclusion**

Zucchini brownies solve two problems at once. They use up a vegetable that tends to pile up faster than anyone can reasonably eat it, and they produce a genuinely better brownie than most standard recipes, egg-free and impossible to overbake into dryness. The batter looks strange halfway through, dry and crumbly in a way that makes you doubt the recipe, and then it comes together into something rich enough that most people won't even ask for frosting.

If you've got a garden producing more zucchini than you know what to do with, or a neighbor handing off bags of it, this is one of the better ways to put it to use. Make one batch and you'll understand why people who bake these once tend to bake them every summer after that.
