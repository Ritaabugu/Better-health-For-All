Zucchini season has a way of sneaking up on you. One week your garden or your farmers market haul looks manageable. The next, you've got more zucchini than any stir fry or side dish rotation can reasonably handle.

Chocolate chip zucchini bread is the answer that doesn't feel like a compromise. It's moist, it's rich with chocolate in every slice, and the zucchini disappears so completely into the batter that most people won't even notice it's there. That last part matters more than you'd think, especially if you're baking for kids who have strong opinions about vegetables showing up where they don't belong.

### **Why Zucchini Works So Well in Baked Goods**

Zucchini brings something most bread recipes are missing: moisture, without adding fat or sugar to get it. Shred it, fold it into the batter, and it quietly does its job while baking. There's no strong vegetable flavor to fight against. No texture that gives it away. Just a loaf that comes out of the oven noticeably more tender than a version made without it.

It's also a practical way to use up a vegetable that tends to overproduce. If you've ever grown zucchini, you already know one or two plants can outpace what any household can eat fresh. Baking it into bread is one of the more forgiving ways to work through a surplus, since a couple loaves can go in the freezer for later.

### **What You'll Need**

This recipe makes one standard loaf, and everything on the list is easy to find at a regular grocery store.

##### **For the dry ingredients:**

* 2 cups all-purpose flour
* ½ teaspoon salt
* ½ teaspoon baking powder
* 1 teaspoon baking soda
* 1 teaspoon cinnamon

##### **For the wet ingredients:**

* 2 large eggs
* 1 cup sugar
* ½ cup vegetable oil
* 2 teaspoons vanilla extract
* 2 cups shredded zucchini

##### **Mix-ins**:

* 1 cup chocolate chips

##### **Equipment**:

* A loaf pan
* Nonstick spray
* Mixing bowls
* A whisk and a spatula
* A box grater, for shredding the zucchini

Eight ingredients beyond the flour and leavening agents, and none of them require a specialty grocery run.

#### **A Note on Shredding Zucchini**

You'll need about two cups of shredded zucchini, which usually works out to one medium zucchini. A standard box grater handles this in a couple of minutes. There's no need to peel it first. The skin softens completely during baking and adds a bit of color without changing the texture.

One thing worth knowing: you don't need to squeeze the moisture out of the zucchini before adding it to the batter. That water is actually doing you a favor here. It helps bind the dry ingredients together and is a big part of why this bread turns out so moist.

### **How to Make Chocolate Chip Zucchini Bread**

The process breaks into three simple stages: mix your dry ingredients, mix your wet ingredients, then bring everything together. None of it requires special equipment or advanced baking skills.

#### **Step 1: Shred the Zucchini**

Start by shredding your zucchini using a box grater until you have about two cups. Set it aside while you prep everything else. This is genuinely the most labor-intensive part of the whole recipe, and it still only takes a few minutes.

#### **Step 2: Combine the Dry Ingredients**

In a mixing bowl, combine the flour, salt, baking powder, baking soda, and cinnamon. Stir them together and set the bowl aside. This is a straightforward step, but getting the leaveners evenly distributed now means your loaf rises evenly later.

#### **Step 3: Whisk the Wet Ingredients**

In a separate large bowl, crack in your two eggs, then add the sugar, vegetable oil, and vanilla extract. Whisk everything together until it's smooth and well combined. You're looking for a uniform, slightly thickened mixture before moving on.

#### **Step 4: Bring It All Together**

Slowly add your dry ingredients into the bowl with your wet ingredients, mixing as you go. At the same time, fold in your shredded zucchini. The moisture from the zucchini helps everything blend together smoothly, so don't worry if the batter looks a little loose at first. That's expected.

Once the dry ingredients and zucchini are fully incorporated, grab a spatula and fold in your chocolate chips. Stop mixing as soon as they're evenly distributed. Overmixing at this stage can make the bread denser than it needs to be.

#### **Step 5: Prepare Your Pan**

Spray a loaf pan with nonstick spray, making sure to coat the sides as well as the bottom. This step is easy to rush, but a poorly greased pan is one of the most common reasons a loaf sticks and tears when you try to remove it later.

#### **Step 6: Pour and Bake**

Pour the batter into your prepared loaf pan and spread it evenly. Bake at 350 degrees Fahrenheit for about an hour. Ovens vary, so start checking around the 50 minute mark. A toothpick inserted into the center should come out clean, or with just a few moist crumbs clinging to it, not wet batter.

### **Tips for the Best Results**

A few small adjustments can make a noticeable difference in how this loaf turns out.

Let the bread cool in the pan for about ten minutes before transferring it to a wire rack. Zucchini bread is delicate straight out of the oven, and rushing this step is a common way to end up with a cracked or crumbly loaf.

Resist the urge to slice it while it's still warm, tempting as that is. The texture sets as it cools, and a loaf sliced too early will seem gummier than it actually is once it's had time to rest.

If your chocolate chips keep sinking to the bottom of the loaf, toss them in a tablespoon of flour before folding them into the batter. This helps them stay suspended throughout the bread instead of settling during baking.

### **Sneaking Vegetables Into Baked Goods**

One of the appealing things about this recipe is how well it hides its main ingredient. Zucchini bread has a long track record of winning over people, kids especially, who wouldn't touch a vegetable on its own plate but have no idea it's baked into their after-school snack.

If you're serving this to picky eaters, there's no real reason to announce what's in it upfront. Let the chocolate chips do the talking. The flavor and texture read as a treat first, and the fact that it's got a vegetable folded in is beside the point once it's out of the oven.

### **Storage**

Once your loaf has cooled completely, store it in an airtight container at room temperature for up to three days. If you want it to last longer, keep it in the fridge, where it'll stay fresh for about a week.

This bread also freezes well, which makes it a good option if your zucchini haul is bigger than what one loaf can use up. Wrap a cooled loaf tightly in plastic wrap, then in foil or a freezer bag, and it'll keep well for up to three months. Thaw it at room temperature before slicing.

### **Serving Ideas**

This bread works well on its own, but a few small additions can turn it into something a little more special. A smear of butter on a warm slice is hard to beat. A light dusting of powdered sugar on top gives it a slightly more finished look if you're bringing it somewhere.

It also holds up as a make-ahead option for busy mornings. Slice it the night before, and you've got a grab-and-go breakfast or snack that doesn't require any extra effort once the day gets going.

### **A Recipe Worth Making Every Zucchini Season**

Chocolate chip zucchini bread earns its place in a regular baking rotation for a simple reason. It solves a seasonal problem, uses ingredients most kitchens already have, and produces a loaf that tastes like a genuine treat rather than a health food compromise.

Whether you're working through a garden overflow or just picked up an extra zucchini at the store without a plan for it, this is a reliable way to put it to good use. One bowl, one pan, about an hour in the oven, and you've got a loaf worth making again before the season's over.
