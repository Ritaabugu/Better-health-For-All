You've got zucchini taking over your kitchen counter again, and this time it's going somewhere better than another batch of muffins. Chocolate zucchini bread turns that pile of summer squash into something genuinely decadent, fudgy in the middle and rich enough that it barely reads as a "vegetable bread" at all.

This isn't a light, health-food version of banana bread. It's a real chocolate dessert that happens to use up zucchini, with a texture closer to brownies than sandwich bread. If you've made a chocolate zucchini cake before, this is the same idea in loaf form, just as satisfying and honestly a little easier to pull off on a weeknight.

### **Chocolate Zucchini Bread**

The secret to this bread's fudgy texture comes down to two things: how the cocoa is prepared and how carefully the zucchini is handled. Blooming the cocoa powder in hot liquid deepens the chocolate flavor in a way that just tossing it in with the dry ingredients never quite achieves. And removing excess water from the zucchini keeps the bread from turning gummy in the middle.

Get those two details right, and everything else about this recipe is refreshingly simple.

#### **What You'll Need**

##### **Dry Ingredients**

* 1½ cups (190g) all-purpose flour
* ⅔ cup (133g) light brown sugar, firmly packed
* ½ cup (100g) granulated sugar
* 1½ teaspoons baking soda
* ¾ teaspoon table salt

##### **Chocolate Mixture**

* ½ cup very hot or boiling water
* ½ cup (50g) Dutch-process cocoa powder
* 1 teaspoon instant coffee (optional)

##### **Wet Ingredients**

* ½ cup melted unsalted butter
* 2 large eggs, room temperature
* 1½ teaspoons vanilla extract

##### **Mix-Ins**

* 2 cups grated zucchini
* 1½ cups semisweet chocolate chips, plus extra for topping

#### **Instructions**

##### **Step 1: Preheat and Prep**

Preheat your oven to 350°F. Line a bread pan with a sling of parchment paper, leaving overhang on the long sides so you can lift the finished loaf out easily. If you'd rather skip the parchment, grease and flour the pan instead.

##### **Step 2: Whisk the Dry Ingredients**

In a large mixing bowl, whisk together the flour, brown sugar, granulated sugar, baking soda, and salt. Break up any clumps in the brown sugar as you go, then set the bowl aside.

##### **Step 3: Bloom the Cocoa Powder**

Pour the very hot water into a separate bowl. Immediately whisk in the cocoa powder and instant coffee, if using. Adding cocoa to hot liquid blooms it, which develops a deeper chocolate flavor than mixing it in dry ever could. If you'd rather skip the instant coffee, leave it out. You could also swap the hot water for strong hot coffee for a similar effect.

##### **Step 4: Add the Butter, Eggs, and Vanilla**

Whisk the melted butter into the cocoa mixture. A little separation is normal here, so don't worry if it looks slightly broken. Lightly beat the eggs with a fork before adding them in, which helps everything combine more smoothly. Whisk in the vanilla extract until the mixture looks glossy and combined.

##### **Step 5: Combine Wet and Dry**

Pour the chocolate mixture into the bowl with your dry ingredients. Using a spatula, gently fold everything together until it's about halfway combined. Be careful not to overmix at this stage, since too much mixing can leave your bread dense or rubbery.

##### **Step 6: Fold in the Zucchini**

Once the batter looks roughly half mixed, fold in the grated zucchini. Adding it partway through, rather than at the very end, lowers the risk of overmixing the batter while still getting the zucchini evenly distributed.

If you're grating your own zucchini, a food processor gives you finer, more consistent texture than a box grater. Shred it first, then pulse briefly with a regular blade attachment until you're happy with the size. Blot the grated zucchini with paper towels afterward to remove excess moisture. You'll notice it start to stick to the towel once enough water has been absorbed.

##### **Step 7: Fold in the Chocolate Chips**

Add the chocolate chips and continue folding until the batter looks uniform. Scrape down the sides and bottom of the bowl as you go. Flour tends to hide in the corners, and a glass bowl makes it easy to check you've gotten it all.

##### **Step 8: Bake**

Spread the batter into your prepared pan. Scatter a few extra chocolate chips on top, knowing some will sink in and some will stay visible.

Bake on the center rack for 85 to 90 minutes. If your pan is a true 9x5, start checking around 77 to 80 minutes, since slightly smaller or darker pans tend to bake faster. Glass pans generally take noticeably longer, so keep that in mind if that's what you're working with.

Test with a wooden skewer rather than a toothpick, since a toothpick often isn't long enough to reach the center properly. You're looking for a few chocolate crumbs and some fudginess on the skewer, not wet batter. Because this bread is loaded with chocolate chips, if your skewer comes out fully chocolatey, you may have simply hit a chip. Test again in a different spot to be sure.

##### **Step 9: Cool and Slice**

Let the bread cool completely in the pan, or at least 30 minutes to an hour, before removing it. While warm, the loaf is prone to falling apart, so cooled slices hold together far better. That said, warm slices taste incredible, so it's really a matter of personal preference.

To remove the loaf, run a butter knife along any edge without parchment paper, then lift using the parchment sling.

#### **Tips for the Best Chocolate Zucchini Bread**

A few small choices make a real difference in how this bread turns out.

Don't skip blooming the cocoa in hot liquid. It's a small extra step that genuinely changes the depth of chocolate flavor in the final loaf, and skipping it leaves the bread tasting flatter than it should.

Blot your zucchini well before adding it to the batter. Excess moisture is one of the most common reasons quick breads like this turn out gummy in the center instead of properly fudgy.

Pay attention to your pan. A glass pan will need considerably more time than a metal one, and a darker metal pan may finish faster than a lighter one. Start checking a few minutes early the first time you make this so you learn how your specific pan behaves.

#### **Storage and Serving Notes**

This bread keeps well at room temperature for a few days if wrapped tightly, and it also freezes nicely if you want to make it ahead. Slice, wrap individual pieces, and pull them out as needed for a quick treat that doesn't require any last-minute baking.

If you love the combination of chocolate and zucchini in this loaf, a chocolate zucchini cake built on the same idea makes an easy follow-up bake for your next gathering.

### **Conclusion**

Chocolate zucchini bread earns its place as one of the best ways to use up a zucchini glut, mostly because it doesn't taste like a consolation prize. It's genuinely rich, fudgy in the middle, and loaded with enough chocolate chips that nobody's thinking about the vegetable folded in.

Make this one the next time your counter is covered in zucchini, and don't be surprised if it disappears faster than anything else you've baked all season.
