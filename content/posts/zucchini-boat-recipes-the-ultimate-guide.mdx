Zucchini boat recipes solve a problem a lot of weeknight dinners run into. You want something high in protein, light on carbs, and filling enough to actually count as dinner, without spending an hour in the kitchen to get there.

This version stuffs zucchini halves with a savory turkey and vegetable filling, tops it with a little melted mozzarella, and bakes until tender. It's the kind of recipe you make once and then keep coming back to all season.

### **Why Zucchini Boat Recipes Are a Weeknight Staple**

The appeal here isn't complicated. Zucchini is naturally low in carbohydrates, and hollowing it out to use as a "boat" gives you a built-in container for a filling that's already doing most of the nutritional heavy lifting. Ground turkey brings the protein, the vegetables bring fiber, and the zucchini itself adds even more bulk without adding many calories.

This particular recipe has been around for over a decade, and it earns its spot in regular rotation for one simple reason. It reheats well, it holds up in the fridge, and it doesn't taste like a "diet" dinner. That combination is rare enough to be worth building your week around.

### **What You Need Before You Start**

#### **Choosing Your Protein**

Ground turkey is the default here, but this recipe is genuinely flexible.

* **Ground turkey**: Lean turkey keeps things high in protein and lower in fat, though it can run a little dry on its own. Dark meat turkey is a good option if you want something juicier.
* **Other ground meats**: Beef, chicken, or pork all work in the same ratio without changing anything else about the recipe.
* **Plant-based swap**: Lentils, chickpeas, mushrooms, quinoa, or crumbled tofu all work well. If you're going this route, sauté your vegetables first, then stir in the plant-based protein once they're cooked.

#### **Ingredients**

##### **For the zucchini boats:**

* 4 medium to large zucchini

##### **For the filling:**

* 1 pound lean ground turkey
* Salt and pepper, to taste
* 2 teaspoons olive oil, divided
* 3 to 4 cloves garlic, chopped
* 1 cup chopped onion
* 1 green bell pepper, chopped
* 1 cup reserved zucchini flesh, roughly chopped
* 1 tablespoon Italian seasoning
* 1½ cups strained tomatoes (crushed tomatoes or tomato sauce also work)

##### **For topping:**

* 1 to 2 tablespoons shredded mozzarella per zucchini half

### **How to Make Stuffed Zucchini Boats, Step by Step**

#### **Step 1: Prep the Zucchini**

Bring a large pot of water to a boil. While it heats, trim the top and bottom off each zucchini, then slice each one in half lengthwise, aiming for a straight cut down the center.

Use a spoon to scoop out the seeds and center flesh, leaving about a quarter inch of zucchini flesh around the edges to hold everything together. You should end up with eight zucchini halves once all four zucchini are prepped.

Take about a cup of the scooped out zucchini center, wrap it in a clean dish towel, and squeeze out the excess water. Give it a rough chop and set it aside, since it goes straight into the filling later. It adds volume and a real boost of fiber. Any leftover scooped zucchini doesn't have to go to waste either. Stash it in an airtight container in the fridge and stir it into a smoothie or scramble it with eggs later in the week.

Drop the zucchini halves into the boiling water for about a minute, then pull them out right away. This quick par boil softens them just enough that they turn out tender after baking. If you prefer a firmer bite, you can skip this step entirely. It comes down to personal preference.

#### **Step 2: Cook the Filling**

Heat a large nonstick pan over medium heat and add a couple teaspoons of olive oil. Add the ground turkey along with a bit of salt and pepper, breaking it up as it cooks until no pink remains.

Push the cooked turkey to one side of the pan and add the remaining olive oil. Add the garlic, onion, bell pepper, and reserved chopped zucchini. Season with more salt and pepper, then stir in the Italian seasoning.

Pour in the strained tomatoes and mix everything together thoroughly. Bring it to a low simmer, cover with a lid, and let it cook for 10 to 15 minutes. Check halfway through, and if it looks dry, stir in a splash more tomatoes to loosen it back up. Once it's done, take it off the heat and let it rest for about five minutes so it's cool enough to handle.

#### **Step 3: Stuff and Top the Zucchini**

Line your zucchini halves up in a baking dish. A 16x10 dish fits about six comfortably, so keep a smaller second dish handy for any extra halves.

Spoon about a quarter to a third of a cup of filling into each zucchini boat, mounding it just slightly above the edges without overfilling. This amount of filling is built to stretch perfectly across all eight halves, so if you've got a little extra at the end, just distribute it across whichever boats have room.

Finish each one with a sprinkle of one to two tablespoons of shredded mozzarella over the top.

#### **Step 4: Bake**

Cover the baking dish with foil. Bake at 350°F for 30 minutes with the foil still on. Once the time is up, remove the foil and you'll find the cheese melted and the zucchini tender, with just enough bite left to hold its shape.

### **Make-Ahead and Meal Prep Tips**

This is one of those zucchini boat recipes that genuinely works better with a plan attached. You can assemble the whole dish, cover it, and refrigerate it for up to 24 hours before baking. Just let it come to room temperature before it goes in the oven, or you'll need to add extra baking time to make up for the cold start.

It also holds up well for meal prep once fully cooked. Make a full tray on Sunday and portion it out across the week. Reheated leftovers taste just as good as the first night, which isn't something every meal prep dinner can claim.

### **Ways to Customize These Zucchini Boat Recipes**

Once you've made the classic version, there's plenty of room to change it up. A taco-inspired version swaps the Italian seasoning for taco seasoning and the mozzarella for a Mexican blend, giving you a completely different flavor profile from the same base technique.

The plant-based route opens up even more variety. Lentils and mushrooms bring a hearty, savory bite that holds up well against the tomato base, while quinoa adds its own texture and a boost of complete protein. Crumbled tofu works too if you're after something closer to the original ground meat texture.

Don't feel locked into ground turkey even if you're sticking with a meat-based filling. Ground chicken, beef, or pork all slot into this same method with zero changes to the technique. The vegetables, tomatoes, and seasoning do most of the flavor work regardless of which protein you choose.

### **Conclusion**

Zucchini boat recipes earn their spot in regular rotation because they hit every mark at once. High in protein, light on carbs, easy to prep ahead, and genuinely satisfying enough that it doesn't feel like a compromise dinner.

Start with the classic turkey and vegetable version above, then branch out once you've got the technique down. Taco style, plant-based, a different ground meat, it's all built on the same simple method. Once you've hollowed out your first batch of zucchini, the rest comes together fast.
