Peach season doesn't last long, and this is exactly what to do with it. Peach cobbler with fresh peaches takes about fifteen minutes of actual work, then the oven does the rest.

This isn't a fussy dessert. There's no pastry to roll out, no crust to blind bake, no waiting overnight for anything to chill. Just ripe peaches, a simple batter, and a baking dish that turns golden and bubbling in under an hour.

## **Why Fresh Peaches Make All the Difference**

A lot of cobbler recipes lean on canned fruit for convenience, and you can absolutely do that here too. But peach cobbler with fresh peaches has a texture and depth of flavor that canned fruit just can't match. Fresh peaches hold their shape a little better and release their juice slowly as they bake, instead of flooding the dish all at once.

The one requirement is ripeness. Hard, underripe peaches won't soften properly and won't give you that jammy, syrupy center that makes this dessert worth making in the first place. If your peaches feel firm, let them sit on the counter for a day or two before you start.

### **What You Need Before You Start**

#### **Choosing Your Peaches**

Fresh is the best option here, but you've got flexibility if fresh isn't available.

* **Fresh peaches**: Six medium peaches, peeled, pitted, and sliced into quarter to half inch pieces. Perfectly ripe is what you're after, not rock hard and not overripe and mushy.
* **Frozen peaches**: A solid backup out of season. Thaw completely, drain off the excess liquid, and pat them dry before using, since frozen fruit holds a lot of extra water.
* **Canned peaches**: Also workable in a pinch. Drain them well first so you're not adding extra liquid to a recipe that already has plenty.
* You don't have to peel your peaches if you don't want to, though the skins can end up a little chewy in the finished dessert. If you're using a very ripe peach, a sharp knife can shave the skin off quickly instead of doing a full peel.

#### **Ingredients**

##### **For the peaches:**

* 6 medium fresh peaches, peeled and sliced
* ¼ cup (50 grams) brown sugar
* ¼ cup (50 grams) granulated sugar

##### **For the topping:**

* 7 tablespoons butter
* 1 cup (120 grams) all-purpose flour
* ¼ teaspoon salt
* 2 teaspoons baking powder
* ½ teaspoon cinnamon
* ¾ cup (about 200 mL) whole milk

### **How to Make Peach Cobbler With Fresh Peaches, Step by Step**

#### **Step 1: Melt the Butter in the Baking Dish**

Preheat your oven to 350°F. Cut the butter into pieces and add it to a 9x13 inch baking dish, then slide the dish into the oven just long enough for the butter to fully melt.

If you want a little more depth of flavor, let the butter bake until it starts to brown slightly before you move on. This step is completely optional, and a straightforward melt works just as well if you're short on time.

#### **Step 2: Prep and Cook the Peaches**

While the butter melts, peel your peaches, remove the pits, and slice them into quarter to half inch pieces. Don't worry about making every slice perfectly even. A few uneven pieces here and there won't matter once everything bakes together.

Add the sliced peaches to a large pan along with the brown sugar and granulated sugar. Cook over medium heat for about two minutes, just until the sugar dissolves completely. If you spot any stubborn clumps of brown sugar, press down on them with your spoon to help them break apart faster. Once the sugar has dissolved, pull the pan off the heat and set the peaches aside.

#### **Step 3: Mix the Cobbler Topping**

In a large bowl, whisk together the flour, salt, baking powder, and cinnamon. Feel free to swap in other warm spices here too, like cardamom or allspice, if you want to change up the flavor slightly.

Pour in the milk and stir just until everything comes together into a smooth batter. Don't overmix it, since a few small lumps are completely fine and won't affect the final texture.

#### **Step 4: Layer and Bake**

Pour the batter directly into the baking dish over the melted butter. Don't stir the two together. Instead, gently spread the batter into an even layer so it forms its own distinct base beneath the fruit.

Spoon the cooked peaches over the batter in an even layer, spreading them out rather than piling them into one thick clump. This matters more than it sounds like it should, because the cobbler topping needs room to rise up through the fruit as it bakes. Bunched up peaches block that rise and leave you with a denser, less fluffy result.

Bake at 350°F for 40 to 45 minutes. You'll know it's ready when the top turns deep golden brown and springs back slightly when you touch it. Let it cool just until it's safe to handle before serving.

### **Common Mistakes to Avoid**

**Using underripe peaches**. Hard peaches won't break down properly in the oven, and you'll end up with tough, undercooked fruit sitting in an otherwise perfect topping. Wait for real ripeness, even if that means buying your peaches a few days ahead.

**Piling the fruit in one spot**. Spread the peaches evenly across the batter instead of dumping them in a single pile. An even layer lets the topping rise through the fruit properly instead of getting trapped underneath it.

**Skipping the sugar cook step**. Cooking the peaches with sugar for those two minutes isn't optional filler. It gives the fruit a head start on releasing its juices and dissolving the sugar, which helps the whole dish bake more evenly.

**Overmixing the batter.** Stir the topping just until it comes together. Overworking it can make the finished cobbler dense instead of light and tender.

**Not draining frozen or canned peaches**. Extra liquid from frozen or canned fruit can leave you with a soggy, underbaked center. Pat fresh-thawed peaches dry, and drain canned ones thoroughly, before adding them to the pan.

### **Ways to Customize This Recipe**

Once you've made peach cobbler with fresh peaches a couple of times, it's an easy recipe to play with. Swap the cinnamon for cardamom or a pinch of nutmeg if you want a slightly different warmth to the spice. A splash of vanilla extract in the batter adds another layer of flavor without changing the texture at all.

If peaches aren't quite in season yet, or you've got other stone fruit on hand, plums or nectarines work in the same ratio without any other changes to the recipe. You can also mix in a handful of blueberries or raspberries alongside the peaches for a slightly different flavor and color in the finished dish.

Serving it is where you can really make it your own. A scoop of vanilla ice cream on top while the cobbler is still warm is the classic move, and there's a reason it's classic. The ice cream melts into the warm fruit and topping, creating that perfect combination of hot and cold in every bite. A dollop of plain whipped cream works nearly as well if you'd rather skip the ice cream altogether.

### **Conclusion**

Peach cobbler with fresh peaches earns its spot as a summer staple because it asks so little and gives back so much. Fifteen minutes of prep, one baking dish, and you've got a dessert that tastes like it took a lot more effort than it did.

Make it while peaches are actually in season and at their best. The flavor difference between a cobbler made with perfectly ripe, in-season fruit and one made with whatever's available in the off-season is significant, and it's worth planning around. Serve it warm, add the ice cream, and don't be surprised when there's nothing left by the end of the night.
