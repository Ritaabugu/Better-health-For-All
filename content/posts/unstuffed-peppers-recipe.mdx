Stuffed peppers are great in theory. In practice, you're hollowing out peppers, cooking a filling separately, stuffing everything back in, then baking it all in the oven for another 40 minutes. It's a lot of steps for a weeknight dinner. This unstuffed peppers recipe cuts out most of that work and gives you the same flavor in one pan.

Instead of stuffing the peppers, you chop them up and cook everything together. Ground beef, rice, tomatoes, and two kinds of cheese all simmer in the same pot until it turns into something closer to a hearty casserole. You get all the flavor of a stuffed pepper without the assembly line.

### **Why This Unstuffed Peppers Recipe Works**

The logic here is simple. Bell peppers, ground beef, rice, and tomatoes taste just as good chopped and mixed together as they do stuffed inside a pepper shell. The shell was never doing much for the flavor anyway. It was mostly just a container.

Skipping the stuffing step also means skipping the extra bake time that comes with cooking raw peppers in the oven. This version cooks everything on the stovetop in about 40 minutes total, and most of that time is hands off simmering. You're not babysitting the stove, and you're not dealing with a sink full of extra dishes either.

It's also easier to customize. Want more peppers and less rice? Adjust it right in the pan. Want it spicier? Add it directly to the mix instead of hoping it seasons evenly through a stuffed pepper. This unstuffed peppers recipe gives you more control with less effort.

### **Unstuffed Peppers Recipe Ingredients**

Here's everything you need for one full pan, enough for four to six servings depending on how hungry everyone is.

* 1 pound ground beef
* 1 red bell pepper, julienned
* 1 yellow bell pepper, julienned
* 1 green bell pepper, julienned
* 1/2 small onion, chopped
* 3 cloves garlic, minced
* 1 teaspoon salt, adjusted to taste
* 2 teaspoons ground black pepper
* 2 teaspoons Italian seasoning
* 1 tablespoon Worcestershire sauce
* 1 (15 oz) can Italian diced tomatoes
* 1 1/2 cups beef broth
* 1 cup long grain rice, uncooked
* 1 cup shredded mozzarella cheese
* 1 cup shredded extra old cheddar cheese
* Fresh parsley, for garnish

Three different colored peppers give this dish a lot of visual variety, but you can use whatever combination you have on hand. A single color works just as well if that's what's in your fridge.

### **How to Make This Unstuffed Peppers Recipe**

#### **Step 1: Brown the Beef**

Heat a bit of oil in a large pan over medium high heat. Add the ground beef and break it up as it cooks, until it's browned through completely.

Drain off most of the excess grease once the beef is fully cooked. You don't need to remove every last bit, but a quick drain keeps the final dish from turning greasy.

#### **Step 2: Prep and Cook the Peppers**

While the beef browns, julienne your bell peppers into thin strips instead of chopping them into small cubes. The strips hold their texture better and give the dish a more distinct pepper presence in every bite.

Once the beef is browned and drained, add the chopped onion and minced garlic to the pan. Saute for a couple of minutes until it turns fragrant. Then add all three peppers and cook for another two minutes, just until they start to soften slightly. A little crunch left in the peppers is a good thing here.

#### **Step 3: Season the Mixture**

Add the salt, black pepper, Italian seasoning, and Worcestershire sauce directly into the pan. Stir everything together so the seasoning coats the beef and vegetables evenly.

This is the step that gives the dish its stuffed pepper flavor profile, so don't rush past it. The Worcestershire sauce especially adds a depth you won't get from salt and pepper alone.

#### **Step 4: Add Tomatoes, Broth, and Rice**

Pour in the canned diced tomatoes along with the beef broth. Stir until everything is combined. The mixture will look soupy at this point, but that's expected since the rice hasn't absorbed any liquid yet.

Bring the mixture to a boil, then stir in the uncooked rice. Give everything one more good stir to distribute the rice evenly throughout the pan.

#### **Step 5: Simmer**

Cover the pan with a lid, reduce the heat to low, and let everything simmer for about 20 minutes. Halfway through, remove the lid and give it a quick stir to make sure nothing is sticking to the bottom, then cover again and continue cooking for the remaining 10 minutes.

By the end of this step, the rice should be tender and most of the liquid should be absorbed.

#### **Step 6: Add the Cheese**

Once the rice is cooked, add half of your mozzarella and half of your cheddar directly into the pan. Stir until the cheese melts into the mixture.

Take the remaining cheese and spread it evenly across the top of the casserole. Cover the pan again, turn the heat down to its lowest setting, and let the cheese melt for about 10 more minutes.

#### **Step 7: Garnish and Serve**

Once the cheese on top has fully melted, remove the lid and let the dish sit for a few minutes before serving. Garnish with fresh parsley for a bit of color and freshness against all that melted cheese.

Serve it straight from the pan. This isn't a dish that needs to look fussy to taste great.

### **Total Time for This Unstuffed Peppers Recipe**

This one runs closer to 40 to 45 minutes total once you factor in browning the beef, simmering the rice, and melting the cheese. That's still faster than traditional stuffed peppers, which often need an hour or more between prep and oven time.

Most of that time is hands off simmering, so you can prep a side dish or just relax while it cooks.

### **Tips for Getting This Recipe Right**

A few small choices make a real difference in how this dish turns out.

Julienne your peppers instead of dicing them. Strips hold up better through the simmering process and give you a more noticeable pepper bite in every forkful, rather than peppers disappearing into the mixture.

Use freshly grated cheese instead of pre-shredded bags if you can. It melts smoother and gives you a creamier finish both inside the casserole and on top.

Don't skip draining the beef. Extra grease left in the pan makes the final dish heavier and greasier than it needs to be.

Keep the lid on during the simmering stage. Removing it too often lets steam escape, which slows down how quickly the rice cooks.

### **Making This Unstuffed Peppers Recipe Part of a Balanced Meal**

This dish already covers a lot of nutritional ground on its own. You've got protein from the ground beef, fiber and vitamin C from three different peppers, and carbohydrates from the rice, all in one pan. That's a fairly complete meal without needing much on the side.

If you want to lighten it up, ground turkey works as a swap for the beef without losing much flavor, since the seasoning is doing a lot of the heavy lifting anyway. Cutting the cheese down to half a cup total instead of two full cups also trims some saturated fat if that's something you're watching.

Portion size matters more than people expect with a dish like this. It's rich enough that a smaller serving alongside a simple green salad often feels just as satisfying as a heaping plate on its own.

### **A Few Notes on Sodium**

Between the beef broth, Worcestershire sauce, and two types of cheese, this dish carries a fair amount of sodium. If that's something you're managing for a health condition, a low sodium beef broth and a lighter hand with the added salt can bring that number down without changing the overall flavor much.

This isn't a dish that needs to be perfect every time you make it. Adjust the seasoning to what works for your body and your taste, and don't feel locked into the exact measurements above.

### **Conclusion**

This unstuffed peppers recipe takes everything people love about stuffed peppers and strips away the parts that make the dish feel like a project. One pan, about 40 minutes, and you end up with something that tastes just as good as the original without the extra cleanup.

It's the kind of recipe that earns a permanent spot in a weekly rotation, not because it's fancy, but because it's genuinely easy to make on a Tuesday night when cooking is the last thing you feel like doing.
