Some desserts earn a permanent spot in your rotation because they're endlessly adaptable. Learn one crumble topping and you can swap the fruit with the seasons for the rest of your life. This time of year, that means peaches and blueberries, and there's a reason this combination shows up in kitchens all summer long.

Peach crumble isn't fussy. There's no pastry crust to fight with, no lattice to weave, just a buttery, craggy topping over fruit that's already doing most of the flavor work on its own. It's the kind of dessert you can make on a Tuesday and still feel proud of.

### **Why This Peach Crumble Works**

The whole dish comes down to two things done well: a topping with real texture, and fruit that isn't drowned in sugar. The topping here is built from cold butter cut into flour and two kinds of sugar, worked just enough that it clumps into big, uneven crumbles rather than a fine sandy mixture. Those crumbles are what give you the golden, craggy top that makes crumble worth making in the first place.

The fruit side stays simple. Peaches, peeled and sliced, tossed with blueberries, a little lemon, sugar, and a touch of flour to thicken the juices as they bake. Nothing here needs to be complicated to taste good.

### **What You'll Need**

##### **For the crumble topping:**

* ¼ pound (1 stick) unsalted butter, very cold, diced small
* 1 cup all-purpose flour
* ⅓ cup granulated sugar
* ¼ cup light brown sugar, lightly packed
* ¼ teaspoon salt
* ¼ teaspoon cinnamon

##### **For the fruit filling:**

* 2 pounds fresh ripe peaches
* 1 to 2 cups fresh blueberries
* 2 teaspoons fresh lemon zest
* 2 tablespoons fresh lemon juice
* ½ cup granulated sugar, adjusted depending on how sweet your peaches are
* ¼ cup all-purpose flour

##### **To serve**:

* Vanilla ice cream (optional, but worth it)

##### **Equipment**:

* Individual ramekins, or one baking dish
* A sheet pan
* Parchment paper

### **How to Make Peach Crumble, Step by Step**

#### **Step 1: Make the Crumble Topping First**

In a bowl, combine the flour, granulated sugar, brown sugar, salt, and cinnamon. Mix well, making sure the brown sugar isn't clumped together.

Add the cold, diced butter and work it into the dry ingredients until the pieces are roughly the size of peas. At that point, press the mixture between your fingers so it clumps into big, rough crumbles rather than staying loose and sandy. It should look messy. That's exactly what you want, since those uneven clumps are what bake up golden and crisp on top.

Set the topping aside while you prep the fruit.

#### **Step 2: Peel the Peaches the Fast Way**

Bring a pot of water to a boil and drop in your peaches for about a minute. Pull them out and plunge them straight into ice water to stop the cooking. The skins will slip right off with almost no effort, which beats trying to peel raw peaches with a knife.

#### **Step 3: Slice and Season the Fruit**

Slice each peeled peach into thick wedges, roughly 8 per peach depending on size. In a large bowl, combine the peach wedges with the lemon zest, lemon juice, sugar, and flour. The flour helps thicken the juices as everything bakes, so you don't end up with a watery crumble.

Gently fold in the blueberries last, taking care not to crush them.

#### **Step 4: Fill Your Dish**

Divide the fruit mixture among individual ramekins, or spread it into a single baking dish if you'd rather serve it that way. Either works.

#### **Step 5: Add the Topping**

Sprinkle the crumble topping over the fruit until it's fully covered, roughly enough to hide the fruit underneath. Don't press it down. Leaving it loose is what gives the topping its craggy, browned edges once it bakes. Pressing it flat leaves you with something closer to a dense crust, which isn't the texture you're after here.

#### **Step 6: Bake**

Place your ramekins or baking dish on a parchment-lined sheet pan. The parchment isn't essential, but it saves you from scrubbing baked-on fruit juice off your pan if things bubble over, which they usually do a little.

Bake at 350°F for 40 to 45 minutes, until the top is deeply golden and the fruit is bubbling at the edges.

#### **Step 7: Let It Rest, Then Serve**

Give the crumble a few minutes to cool before serving so the juices have a chance to settle. Serve warm, ideally with a scoop of vanilla ice cream melting into the fruit underneath. It's a small addition that makes a noticeable difference.

### **A Genuinely Useful Make-Ahead Tip**

Here's the detail that makes this recipe worth keeping around: the assembled, unbaked crumble holds well in the refrigerator before it ever sees the oven. That means you can prep a full batch, bake off two servings for tonight, and save the rest for later in the week without doing any extra work when the craving hits again.

This is especially handy with individual ramekins. Bake exactly as many as you need and leave the rest covered in the fridge until you're ready.

### **A Few Ways to Change This Up**

**Swap the fruit with the season.** The topping in this recipe works with almost any fruit. Apples make a classic crisp in the colder months, and pears layer in well alongside them for something a little softer. Cranberries add a tart contrast when mixed with apple and pear in the fall.

**Try raspberries with peaches**. If blueberries aren't what you've got on hand, raspberries pair just as well with peaches and bring a sharper, slightly tart edge to the filling.

**Adjust the sugar to your fruit**. Very ripe, sweet peaches need less added sugar. Firmer or slightly underripe fruit can handle the full amount without tasting overly sweet.

**Make it one big dish instead of individual servings**. If ramekins aren't part of your kitchen setup, this recipe works just as well baked in a single dish. Expect the bake time to run a little longer, closer to 50 minutes, since the fruit layer will be deeper.

### **Conclusion**

Peach crumble earns its spot as a go-to summer dessert because it asks so little and delivers so much. No pastry skills required, no finicky technique, just cold butter, ripe fruit, and an oven doing most of the work. Once you've made the topping a couple of times, you'll find yourself reaching for this recipe well past peach season, swapping in whatever fruit happens to be at its best.

Make a full batch, bake what you need tonight, and know the rest is waiting in the fridge for whenever the craving comes back around.
