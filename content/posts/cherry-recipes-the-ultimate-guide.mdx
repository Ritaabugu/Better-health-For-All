You bought too many cherries again. They looked perfect at the store, and now there's a two pound bag sitting in your fridge with no plan attached. That's exactly what this guide is for.

Cherry recipes have a reputation for being fussy, mostly because of the pitting. But once you get past that one messy step, this is one of the most forgiving desserts you'll ever make. We're building a full, from scratch cherry pie: flaky butter crust, thick jammy filling, golden lattice top. No canned filling. No shortcuts that leave you with a soggy bottom.

## **Why This Cherry Pie Recipe Actually Works**

Most cherry recipes tell you to dump canned filling into a store bought crust and call it a day. That's fine if you're in a hurry, but it tastes like it. The filling is often gluey, and the crust rarely holds up.

This version does two things differently. The pie dough gets folded and layered by hand instead of just mixed, which builds flaky layers into the crust itself. And the filling is cooked down on the stove first, so you control exactly how thick it gets before it ever touches the oven. That's the difference between a pie that slices clean and one that turns into cherry soup on the plate.

### **What You Need Before You Start**

Good cherry recipes start with good cherries, and there's more flexibility here than you'd think.

#### **Picking Your Cherries**

Fresh, frozen, or canned all work, and each one changes the process slightly.

* **Fresh cherries**: Red cherries give you the classic look, but Rainier or black cherries work too. Budget real time for pitting, especially if you don't own a cherry pitter.
* **Frozen cherries:** Thaw them fully and drain the liquid, but don't toss it. That juice carries flavor you'll want to use later.
* **Canned cherries:** The fastest option, though the filling below is built for fresh or frozen fruit and tastes noticeably better.
* If your cherries taste bland, and sometimes they do even when they look gorgeous, add a bit more sugar plus some lemon zest to wake the flavor back up.

#### **Pie Dough Ingredients**

* 300 grams (2½ cups) all purpose flour
* ¼ teaspoon salt
* ¼ cup (50 grams) granulated sugar
* 1 cup very cold butter, cubed
* About 6 tablespoons ice water

#### **Cherry Filling Ingredients**

* 4 cups (about 650 grams) pitted cherries
* ½ cup (100 grams) granulated sugar, plus ½ cup more for the cornstarch mixture
* 2 tablespoons fresh lemon juice
* 50 grams (5 tablespoons) cornstarch
* 2 tablespoons butter
* ½ teaspoon almond extract (optional)

#### **For Finishing**

* 1 egg plus 1 tablespoon milk or cream, for the egg wash
* Coarse or sanding sugar, for sprinkling

### **How to Make This Cherry Recipe, Step by Step**

#### **Step 1: Build the Dough by Hand**

Whisk the flour, salt, and sugar together in a large bowl. Scatter the cold butter cubes over the top, then work them in with your hands until you've got a mix of pea sized crumbs and larger chunks. A pastry cutter works too if your hands run warm.

Add the ice water a little at a time. You'll know you've added enough once a handful of dough holds together when you squeeze it. Don't knead it. Instead, press the dough forward with the heel of your hand and fold it over itself a few times. This builds the same flaky layers that make bakery crust so good.

Divide the dough in half. Shape one half into a disc for the bottom crust and the other into a rough rectangle, since you'll be cutting that one into strips later. Wrap both pieces and chill for at least 30 minutes. You can also make the dough a full day ahead.

#### **Step 2: Cook Down the Filling**

While the dough chills, add your pitted cherries to a medium pot with ½ cup sugar and the lemon juice. Cook over medium heat, stirring often, until the mixture is bubbling and the cherries have released their juice.

Whisk the cornstarch with the remaining ½ cup sugar in a small bowl, then stir it into the bubbling cherries. The mixture will look cloudy for a minute before it thickens into a glossy, jammy filling. Stir in the butter and, if you're using it, the almond extract. Once it's thick enough to coat a spoon, pull it off the heat and let it cool to room temperature. Hot filling will melt your pie dough on contact, so don't rush this part.

#### **Step 3: Roll, Fill, and Lattice**

Preheat your oven to 425°F. Roll the bottom dough into a 13 inch circle and ease it into a 9 inch pie dish without stretching it, since stretched dough shrinks back during baking. Roll the top dough slightly thicker and cut it into inch wide strips for the lattice.

Pour in your cooled filling. Lay the longest strips down the center first, then weave the remaining strips over and under to build the lattice pattern. Fold the bottom crust's overhang up and over the lattice ends, then crimp the edge with your fingers or a fork.

#### **Step 4: Egg Wash and Bake**

Brush the entire top with the egg and milk mixture, covering every exposed piece of dough so nothing bakes up pale. Sprinkle coarse sugar over the top for crunch and shine.

Bake at 425°F for 20 minutes, then lower the temperature to 375°F and bake another 30 to 40 minutes, until the filling is bubbling and the crust is deep gold. If the edges are browning too fast, tent them with foil for the last stretch of baking. Let the pie cool before slicing, or the filling won't hold its shape.

### **Ways to Make This Cherry Recipe Your Own**

This base recipe is flexible enough to handle a few swaps without falling apart.

Out of cherries, or just want to experiment? Other stone fruits work in the same filling, as long as you don't slice them too thin. Thin slices break down into mush during baking, and nobody wants that. A blend of cherries and a late summer peach is worth trying if you've got both on hand.

If you're working with frozen cherries and the filling seems thin after cooking, that saved juice from thawing can be reduced separately and stirred back in for extra flavor without extra water. And if pitting by hand sounds miserable, it kind of is. A cherry pitter is one of those cheap kitchen tools that earns its keep the first time you use it.

### **Conclusion**

Cherry recipes get overcomplicated for no good reason. Once you've made this pie once, you'll see how much of it is just waiting: waiting for the dough to chill, waiting for the filling to cool, waiting for that first golden slice to set. The hands on work is actually pretty simple.

Make it once with fresh cherries in the summer, then again with frozen ones in the middle of winter when you're craving something that tastes like July. Either way, you'll end up with a pie that beats anything from a can, and a lattice top that looks like you spent way more effort than you actually did.
